# Ship records — four routes, and one idea nobody has tried

David asks: *could there be anything on ship records?* Yes — but probably **not the kind of ship record
we have been assuming**.

## The assumption worth dropping
We have been looking for a **passenger**. A young Argentine horseman reaching the Cape between 1899 and
1905 may never have bought a passenger ticket at all.

## *** THE IDEA: he came with the horses ***
The South African War (1899–1902) shipped roughly **360,000 horses** into the country. **Argentina was one
of the main sources** — British officers complained about the "mongrel Argentines" and set Australian
bushmen to breaking them.

**Pablo Armando was 17 when that war began and 20 when it ended.** He was a horseman by trade, and by 1911
he was a *horse-dealer* at the Cape. A man of exactly that age and trade, moving from the River Plate to
Cape Town in exactly those years, was moving **with a cargo**.

Horses do not travel alone. Every transport carried **conductors, grooms and muleteers**, engaged at the
port of loading — and those men appear in **military and remount paperwork**, not in civilian passenger
lists. **That is why he may be invisible in the passenger indexes.**

### Where that paperwork is
- **The National Archives, Kew** — **Remount Department** records, principally **WO 108** (South African
  War) and related WO series: purchasing commissions abroad, shipping returns, depot registers.
- The **remount depots at the Cape**, chiefly **Stellenbosch**, and their registers in the South African
  archives.
- Argentine purchasing-commission correspondence: British buyers operated at Buenos Aires, and both sides
  wrote it down.

**Nobody has looked at any of this.** It is the largest untried idea in the project and it fits every fact
we hold about him.

## The three conventional routes, in order of promise
1. **CEMLA** — arrivals into Buenos Aires, 1882–1960. Free, CAPTCHA-gated. See
   `requests/cemla-ship-search.md`. **Finds his parents, not him.**
2. **UK outward passenger lists, BT 27** — survive from 1890, indexed on FindMyPast and Ancestry
   (subscription; not reachable from here). Worth it **only** if he transshipped through Southampton.
   Remember he and Mary Septima were **not married until 1911**, so search her as **TAYLOR** and expect
   them to travel **separately**.
3. **South African inbound passenger lists** — Cape Town arrivals, in the Western Cape archives and
   NAAIRS. Poorly indexed; needs a name and a year to be worth the search.

## The spellings to use, every time
**LERENA · LLERENA · LARENA · LERINA** — all four are attested for this man or his family.
