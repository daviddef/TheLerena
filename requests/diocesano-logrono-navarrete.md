**Target:** the parish registers of **NAVARRETE, La Rioja** — baptisms and marriages, c. 1820–1890.

## Why a letter at all

**FamilySearch has not filmed them.** The catalogue for Navarrete, Logroño returns **one subject —
Census — and one item**, the Catastro de Ensenada of 1752, and the full-text corpus holds no Navarrete
volume of any kind. A full-text sweep for the surname against the town returns Llerena of Badajoz over
and over and nothing local. There is no route to these books from a desk.

## Why Navarrete

It is **the one place this archive can name where LERENA y LERENA was settled local usage** rather than
an indexer's doubling. The evidence is a single document, made 10,000 km away: a power of attorney
signed at the **Spanish consulate in Buenos Aires on 14 November 1900** (folio 138, escritura nº 368),
in which

- **Don PRUDENCIO BENITO** and **Don JUAN JOSÉ LERENA Y LERENA**, brothers, of age, bachelors, shop
  assistants, ***naturales de Navarrete, provincia de Logroño***, grant power to
- their mother **Doña ISABELA LERENA Y BASARÁN**, widow, and mention a third brother,
- **Don JOSÉ DOLORES LERENA Y LERENA**, married, the *propietario* at home.

Juan José was born about 1876. **The children carry the name doubled as a surname of record**, which
means the father and the mother were both Lerena. In every American register this archive has read, that
combination is a transcription fault — forty-three of them tested on 14 September, every one a machine
doubling, and a forty-fourth found on 12 October at Guadalajara. Navarrete is the only candidate left
where it may be real.

## The three things asked

1. Do the **Navarrete parish registers** (baptisms and marriages), c. 1820–1890, survive in the Archive
   and are they open to consultation?
2. Is there a **marriage of a LERENA to a LERENA** at Navarrete before about 1876 — presumably the
   parents of the three brothers, the bride Isabela Lerena y Basarán, a widow by 1900?
3. Are there **baptisms** for Prudencio Benito, Juan José and José Dolores Lerena y Lerena, c. 1870–80?

The letter asks for the procedure and any fee schedule rather than for a search, and offers to pay.

## What it would and would not settle

It would **not** name Juan Carlos Lerena. Nothing about this household is evidence of Pablo Armando's
parents, and the letter does not pretend otherwise. What it would settle is whether the doubled surname
that makes his enrolment card look strange was **an ordinary thing somewhere** — which changes how the
card should be read, and is the reason open question 1 has called Navarrete "worth a day" since
11 October 2026.

## Also filmed, and reachable without anybody's help

The **Catastro de Ensenada for Navarrete, 1752** — DGS **4386870** (vols 481–484) and **4386703**
(vol 485, *Memoriales de seglares*), from the Archivo Histórico Provincial de Logroño. It cannot name a
man born in the 1840s, but it can say whether LERENA was a Navarrete surname at all before the
nineteenth century. That is a separate errand and does not wait on this letter.
