The Marriages

Who married whom, and what this archive has decided about everybody else who carries the name. A shared surname is not a relationship — this page is the record of which ones are.

Why this page is grouped the way it is

This archive's subject is a name, not a family. Most of the people below carry some spelling of Lerena and have no proved relationship to the line at all — and sorting out which do is the entire reason the archive exists. So they are not grouped by descent, which would be a claim the evidence cannot support, and not by surname, which is the very thing in question. They are grouped by what this archive has decided about each of them.

8 people are excluded outright — Lola Lerena, Italian-born, where the name is coincidental; Catherine Buys, where «Lerena» is a given name and not the surname at all. An archive that grouped on the name would have swallowed all 8. They are kept visible rather than deleted, so the next person to search this name finds the answer instead of doing the work again.

What would move somebody up this page

  • A marriage act. It names both fathers, which is the one record that can turn an unplaced Lerena into a connected one.
  • An Argentine civil registration. Most of the unplaced carry a birth year and a nationality and nothing else.
  • A passenger list with an age and a companion. Several of the of interest group are couples travelling together — a relationship the shipping lists record and no register has yet confirmed.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena
