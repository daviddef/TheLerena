Every change to this archive is committed with the reason, not just the result. That habit costs nothing at the time and is worth a great deal later: it means the record of why a thing was believed survives alongside the thing itself.

A tenth dispensation, missed two ways at once, and a seventh spelling

did Found by searching the rarest name in the branch - AMBROSIA TRAIBEL - rather than the surname. That is now the fifth major find in two days that came from refusing to search LERENA. CATEDRAL BASILICA DE MONTEVIDEO, 3 FEBRUARY 1892. "dispensados por el Ilustrisimo y Rmo Senor Obispo Diocesano Monsenor Doctor Don MARIANO SOLER del impedimento canonico de parentesco de TERCER GRADO DE CONSANGUINIDAD CON ATINGENCIA AL SEGUNDO ... contrajo Don ALFREDO GERENA, natural de esta Republica, soltero, de treinta y cinco anos, hijo legitimo de Don AVELINO LORENA y Dona AMBROSIA TRAIBEL. Con Dona HERMENEGILDA CAROLINA GAVARRO ... fueron testigos Don Carlos Navia y Dona AMBROSIA TRAIBEL." ALFREDO LERENA TRAIBEL, born about 1857 - a second child for Avelino and a brother of Josefa. His mother stood formal witness at his wedding. The couple were COUSINS, and the dispensation was granted personally by MARIANO SOLER, the first Archbishop of Montevideo. AND THE SWEEP MISSED IT TWO WAYS AT ONCE. This morning the dispensation file said EIGHT Lerena dispensations and "every Lerena marries out". A ninth turned up at midday - Ema Sixta's, invisible because her COMPOUND surname sat between the two words of the search formula. This is a tenth, and it hid differently again: 1. The groom is written GERENA - a SEVENTH spelling for this family, after Lerena, Llerena, Lorena, Serena, Lerina and Sancio. And his own father is written LORENA in the same sentence, by the same priest. 2. The dispensation clause comes BEFORE the names, so the formula "Lerena el impedimento" - which assumes the surname precedes the word - cannot match. So the count of eight has been breached twice in one day and is certainly low. The file says so. WHAT SURVIVES IS NARROWER AND STILL WORTH SOMETHING: no LERENA-LERENA dispensation has been found - Alfredo married a Gavarro. But this family DID marry kin, at the third degree touching the second, in the generation before Juan Carlos and Maria. That does not answer the standing question. It does establish that the thing being asked about happened here. Register: 234 -> 239 people.

ALEX assessed: its search is broken, and the controls prove it

did David asked whether anything on alex.onb.ac.at - the Austrian National Library's historical law and gazette collection, about five million pages - could relate to the Lerenas. Checked rather than guessed. THE SEARCH RETURNS ZERO FOR EVERYTHING. Its content search gives "Suchergebnisse TOTAL (0)" together with "Diese Seite steht zur Zeit leider noch nicht zur Verfugung" for every query. LERENA returns nothing - and so do "Konsul", "Uruguay" and "Montevideo", each in the identical 166-byte response. A term certain to appear in five million pages of Austrian law returning zero is not a null. It is a broken search, and this archive documented exactly that failure mode on FamilySearch two days ago. The controls took a minute and are the only reason the zero was not written up as an answer. THE HONEST ASSESSMENT, INDEPENDENT OF THE OUTAGE. The prior is low: this family is Uruguayan, Argentine, Spanish, then South African and British, and four days of records touch the Habsburg lands nowhere. THE ONE REAL HOOK IS CONSULAR. The standing open question is whether Pablo Armando was a Consul General, and consular appointments are gazetted. Austria-Hungary kept consulates at Montevideo and Buenos Aires and listed its consuls in the Hof- und Staatshandbuch. If any Lerena held an AUSTRO-HUNGARIAN honorary consulship in the River Plate, ALEX could show it - and this family is exactly the class honorary consuls were drawn from: a president at a font, a general, a judge, an abogado. It would NOT reach Pablo Armando's own claimed consulship, which was ARGENTINE and at the Cape. That needs the South African gazette, already on the list. Logged as an errand to retry when the search recovers, with what to search for. And a note recorded but not acted on: ALEX is likely far more use to the DEFRANCESKI side - Istria, Kvarner and Carniola were Habsburg, and Austrian Landesgesetzblatter are the right law for them. That is this project's sibling archive and the two are kept strictly apart.

FamilySearch's tree attaches that woman to profile K4VN-52H - "Josefa Lerena Traibel, 1851-1947" - the same candidate. If that is her, she was "de Moratorio", and "de L." on the census line is the enumerator's shorthand for THIS HOUSEHOLD - the Lerena house - written against every married woman in it. WHAT I READ RIGHT AND WHAT I DID NOT. Re-examined at DeepZoom level 13: line 4 does read "Lerena de L. Josefa", and the "de L." is formed identically to the two lines above. The LETTER was read correctly. The INFERENCE was too confident. Lines 2 and 3 are genuinely "de Lerena" - Justiniana, widow of Luis Lerena, and Julia Salvanach, wife of Gilberto - so both readings fit them equally, and neither of them tests the shorthand at all. I had no control case and did not look for one before publishing. THE TIERING, PLAINLY. The Moratorio marriage records are INDEX-LEVEL civil registration - real records. The link from that Josefa to Josefa Lerena Traibel is a TREE ATTACHMENT, which is SECONDARY and exactly the kind of claim this archive refuses to build on. So neither side of this is settled: what is settled is that my census reading is not evidence. The claim never reached a narrative page - it lived in the register, a data file and a commit message, and the changelog carries that commit as history. The living-people rule now gates this build

did David's rule across the estate: a LIVING PERSON IS NAMED AND NOTHING MORE — no date of birth, no date of death, no place. And his age rule: anyone more than eighty years old is treated as deceased, because an archive that cannot say whether somebody is alive has to presume something and that is what it presumes. Until now Falco was the only archive with a guard, and it had one because the same date leaked three times — twice from code that read correctly in the source. Every one was caught by reading the BUILT HTML. Its design is now in the kit and runs here. It takes the full date strings the DATA attaches to a living person and asserts those literal strings appear nowhere in the build. Falco's two earlier designs are not repeated: bare years pass vacuously on a genealogy site, and name-near-date fires on every homonym. Identity lives in the data, not the markup. An archive that omits living people altogether passes with nothing to check, which is correct rather than a gap — that rule is stricter than this one. Verified by planting a guarded date in a build and watching it fail.
