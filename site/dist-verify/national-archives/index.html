The UK National Archives

The British archives answer

On 17 September 2026 the UK National Archives was searched for the first time in this project. The Lerena surname is absent from Britain. The wife's line is not: the Brayleys fill 639 records, and five men of Northam and Bideford came back with their exact dates of birth.

Documented Discovery, the catalogue of The National Archives, searched 17 September 2026. It is free and needs no account, and it aggregates roughly 2,500 UK repositories rather than Kew alone — on the control query, Kew held 4,791 records and other archives 11,719, most of them the North Devon Record Office.

The control was run first. Bideford returns 16,510 records, so every zero on this page is a tested zero rather than a search that failed.

The surname is absent from Britain

Lerena returns two records, and neither is a person of this family — an 18th-century street plan of Seville dedicated to a Spanish finance minister, and a 1915 crew list that matches incidentally. Llerena returns one Peninsular War letter about the Spanish town of that name. Lerina returns two, both incidental.

This is what it should look like. A family that went from Rosario to the Cape without ever touching Britain leaves nothing in British archives, and now that has been tested rather than assumed.

A CANDIDATE, AND IT LANDS ON AN OPEN DISCREPANCY. This archive has John Walker Brayley born 1845 at St Paul's, London - which IS Middlesex - and has already logged that his death index gives age 53 in Dec qtr 1900, implying about 1847, a two-year gap left open. Aged 18 in 1861 implies about 1843. Three sources, three different years, and a Royal Marines attestation states date and place of birth outright. And the militia line is the strongest thing here: a man born in Middlesex who was serving in a South Devon regiment is a Devon family's son born in London, which is exactly this family's shape. Still not asserted as him - order the document.

School admission registers name the child, the date of birth, the father and THE ADDRESS - and Lucy Elizabeth (b.1881), Mary Septima (b.1884) and Agnes Walker (b.1889) were all of school age inside 1885-1949. It does not confirm 23 Geneva Place for Horace, born 1872 and too old for these registers. It gives the address question a route it did not have.
Septima Taylor / Horace Archibald Taylor / Isabella Temple Taylor 1 / 8 / 19 Every hit is a document that merely contains the words - crew lists, a Metropolitan Police leavers' register, an 18th-century division list. THE LIMIT OF THIS SOURCE, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY SINGLE HIT. Discovery does not name-index ordinary civilians. It indexes people only where a SERIES was catalogued name by name - ADM 157 and ADM 188 for the services, PROB and IR 26 for wills, ADM 73, AIR 76 and AIR 79. Searching a civilian's full name here will return noise and must never be written down as a null about that person. The Brayley results above are catalogue entries for deeds and wills, not a family index.

The naval sweep, and a near miss that is now closed

Northam parish contains Appledore, so if the Taylor men went to sea the Royal Navy registers should hold them. ADM 188 is indexed man by man and gives an exact date of birth, which makes it testable against a tree instead of merely suggestive.

It returned five men of these two parishes. Not one of them is a man of this family — and that the method found five is precisely what makes the absence of William John and Horace Archibald Taylor a tested null.

Taylor, William 14 October 1871, Northam ADM 188/195/140021, Royal Navy register of seamen's services - THE DOCUMENT ITSELF, read 17 September 2026. O.N. 140021, Devonport. Born 14 Oct 1871, Northam. The catalogue rejection rested on a census age; the record gives two harder reasons. FIRST, the form asks for 'Names in full' and the answer is 'William Taylor' - our man is William JOHN Taylor, and the Navy had no reason to drop a middle name it had asked for. SECOND, AND IT IS CONCLUSIVE: on census night, 5 April 1891, this man was borne on HMS Impregnable at Devonport - he joined her 20 May 1890 and left 4 October 1892 - while William John Taylor was enumerated at home in Northam, aged 20. One man cannot be in two places on one night. They are two men. The near miss is closed.

The Taylors of Appledore, in the merchant service 1846, 1888, 1893 Crew list of the Ulelia, official number 74429, National Maritime Museum: T Taylor, Mate, b.1846 Appledore; J W Taylor, Master, b.1888 Appledore; T G Taylor, Ordinary Seaman and Cook, b.1893 Appledore; previous ship Annie of Bideford. A SEAFARING TAYLOR FAMILY IN THE SAME PARISH, AND NOT ONE DOCUMENT JOINS THEM TO OURS. Appledore is in Northam. The generations are wrong for our men - ours are 1870 and 1872 - and no relationship is asserted. Recorded as context, and as a warning: a Taylor at sea out of Northam is not by itself our Taylor.
WILLIAM JOHN TAYLOR (b. Nov 1870) and HORACE ARCHIBALD TAYLOR (b. Apr 1872) - NOTHING. A TESTED NULL, AND A USEFUL ONE. Both parishes were swept, both spellings of the birthplace, and the series returned five other men by name and date while returning neither of these two. They were not Royal Navy ratings. If they went to sea it was the merchant service - and merchant crew lists are indexed by SHIP, not by man, which is a far harder search and should be costed before it is started.
HOW THIS WAS READ, AND THE WALL THAT CAME DOWN HALFWAY - Discovery's JSON API, then Discovery's own web search. The API answered 200 for about twenty queries and then began returning 202 with an empty body and the header `x-amzn-waf-action: challenge`. That is AWS bot detection, and it was not worked around: the control query itself started failing, so every zero collected at that moment was thrown away rather than recorded. The rest was done in a browser at reading pace, which is the access the catalogue is built for. Anyone scripting this again should expect the challenge after roughly twenty rapid calls and slow down well before it.
WHAT COSTS MONEY AND WHAT DOES NOT - ADM 188/195/140021 and the rest. The catalogue entry - name, exact date of birth, birthplace, year of entry - is free, and it is the part that does the genealogical work. The scanned service record is £3.50, or free to a signed-in TNA account, and shows ships, conduct and physical description. A watermarked preview is offered in their viewer and the image cannot be fetched outside it. No account was created and nothing was bought; that is David's call.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.
