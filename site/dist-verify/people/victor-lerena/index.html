Victor LERENA (d.1889, alpargatero)

A WHOLE BRANCH OF LERENAS THIS ARCHIVE HAD NEVER SEEN, found 15 September 2026 by searching FIDEL rather than the surname. His death registration: "ha fallecido VICTOR LERENA de nacionalidad oriental de veinte y ocho anos de edad, de estado soltero y de profesion ALPARGATERO, a consecuencia de TUBERCULOSIS PULMONAR ... el finado era hijo [de] PEDRO LORENA y JOSEFA SEGUEIRA, orientales, FINADOS. Abuelos paternos FIDEL LERENA y MARIA RUEDAS; maternos Jose Seguera y Ramona." He died in hospital at twenty-eight, single, of tuberculosis, and the declaration was made DE OFICIO - by the authorities, not by family. An alpargatero is a maker of rope-soled shoes. Every other Lerena this archive has placed in Montevideo is a hacendado, a juez de paz, a major or a senator's family. This is the same surname at the other end of that city. WHETHER THIS FIDEL IS SANCHO'S SON IS NOT ESTABLISHED. Sancho's Fidel was born about 1799 and the Geneanet trees give him a death in 1822, at twenty-two or twenty-three. For this to be the same man, Pedro would have to be born by 1822 and fathering Victor at about forty. That is tight but possible, and the tree's 1822 is tree-only and has never been tested against a Montevideo burial. The alternative is a second Fidel Lerena at Montevideo, which nothing rules out. Recorded as a branch to test, not as a descent. READ OFF THE PLATE 21 September 2026, where this row had rested on a MACHINE TRANSCRIPT. Film 008165116 image 01442, folio 94 - Montevideo civil death registration No. 622 of 1889. THE ACT GIVES A CAUSE OF DEATH THE TRANSCRIPT NEVER CARRIED: 'a consecuencia de TUBERCULOSIS PULMONAR, segun consta del certificado medico'. His trade is confirmed in the priest's - the registrar's - own hand: ALPARGATERO. And the registration is 'de oficio', an official declaration rather than one made by kin, which is why no relative signs it. THE ACT RUNS TO THREE GENERATIONS: 'el finado era hijo de PEDRO LERENA y JOSEFA SEGUEIRA, orientales, FINADOS. Abuelos [paternos] FIDEL LERENA y MARIA RUEDAS: MATERNOS JOSE SEGUEIRA y RAMONA...'

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.

An evidence-first family archive for the Lerena family — of Rosario and the River Plate, of the Cape and the Rand, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
