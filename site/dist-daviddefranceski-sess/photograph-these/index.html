6 places · all of them in Cape Town

Photograph these

Everything this archive would like a picture of, and cannot take itself. Each says what the photograph would settle, and what to capture — because a photograph of the wrong part of a thing is a wasted trip.

All six are within half an hour of each other. Someone with a morning and a camera could close the first two.

In order of what the picture would settle

In order of what the picture would settle

1

Maitland, Woltemade Cemetery, Cape Town - album "Le", image 85 of 127 in the eGGSA collection, photographed 2021 by Heather MacAlister

The LERENA grave kerb

What to capture: The WHOLE plot, not just the kerb: any plate or slab, the section and row markers, the neighbouring graves, and a wide shot showing where it sits. Then ask the municipal cemetery office for the Woltemade register entry - a grave number would tie the kerb to a person.

The only physical trace of this family found anywhere. The kerb carries the surname and NOTHING else - no forename, no dates. Both Mary Septima (grave 7719A, Nov 1935) and Pablo Armando (Maitland Cemetery no 1, Mar 1950) are buried in this cemetery.

2

Cape Town, Wynberg district

7 Forth Road, Newlands

What to capture: The street frontage, the number, and enough of the street to place it.

Where he died on 19 March 1950, and where his executrix Rose Lilian Theys - "no relation" - also lived. The house is very likely still standing.

3

Cape Town, near Milnerton

The stables site, Koeberg Road, Rugby

What to capture: Whatever is on the site now, and the road. Even a negative - a warehouse, a car park - is worth recording.

Plans were lodged in 1931 for stables here - KAB 3/CT 4/2/1/3/474 B796. The direct documentary corroboration of the horse tradition, and the yard where Alan Higgins and Peter Kannemeyer were apprenticed to "Bob Lerena".

4

Cape Town

34 Florence Road, Observatory

What to capture: The frontage and the number.

Where Mary Septima died in November 1935.

5

Cape Town

St Dominic's, Wynberg

What to capture: The exterior, and the interior if it is open.

Where they married on 3 September 1911, after banns.

6

Cape Town

Corpus Christi, Wynberg

What to capture: The exterior.

Where Roque was baptised on 12 June 1911, three months before his parents' wedding.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.

An evidence-first family archive for the Lerena family — of Rosario and the River Plate, of the Cape and the Rand, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
