The spine, generation by generation

The Spine

The descent from Pablo Armando is closed and documented, with no inferred link left in it. And as of 10 September 2026 the line runs one generation higher: an Argentine army card names his father and mother.

Two things shape this page. Living people are omitted from the build, which removes the most recent generations. And the top of the line changed on 10 September 2026: an Argentine army enrolment card, filled in at the consulate in Cape Town, names Pablo Armando’s father and mother. For months there was nothing above him. There is now one generation, and it is documented rather than inferred. The card → · Method → · What has been searched →

Her line goes back further than his

Documented Pablo Armando's wife was Mary Septima Taylor (17 September 1884 – 15 November 1935), and her family can be traced three generations above her, to a James Taylor born about 1796 — still further back than the Lerena side, where Pablo Armando's parents are now named but nothing beyond their names is known.

Her name is a count: she was the seventh daughter of ten children, and Septima means seventh. The Taylors →

The Chappell–Forbes knot

A correction to this archive’s own record, and an arrangement that reads strangely until the dates are put in order.

This archive recorded Roque’s first wife as “Rosalina Wilhelmina Chappell”, taken from a marriage index, and Ricardo’s wife as “Doreen ?”, taken from the family chart. Both were wrong in the same way, and one document fixes them.

Roseline Wilhelmina FORBES married Frederick Christian Chappell on 1 November 1920 at Woodstock, Cape Town — an event recorded twice over, in the South African civil marriage records and in the Anglican parish registers, and confirmed again by Frederick’s own Western Cape estate file. So Chappell was her married name, not her maiden name. She was a Forbes.

Frederick Chappell died in 1933. Three years later his widow married Roque Luis Armando Lerena, which is why the 1936 register calls her Rosaline Wilhermina Forbes Chappell — exactly what a remarrying widow would be called. And her eldest daughter by Chappell, Doreen May, born 1921, married Roque’s brother Ricardo Juan Carlos Lerena.

Two Lerena brothers married a mother and her daughter. Ricardo’s wife was his brother’s stepdaughter; Roque’s wife was his brother’s mother-in-law. Doreen’s three siblings became Roque’s stepchildren in 1936.

Inferred What is not proved: that this Doreen May Chappell is that Doreen. The relationships come from the FamilySearch Family Tree, which is user-contributed and is not evidence on its own. Every surrounding fact checks out against records; the link itself rests on the tree plus the surname appearing in both marriages. Strong, but not a document, and marked as such.

A discrepancy left standing. Doreen’s death is 17 November 1993 in the tree, and the Johannesburg cemetery register agrees exactly on day and month — but this archive’s own Government Gazette sweep found her estate advertised on 23 February 2001. Either the year is 1999, or the estate sat unadvertised for six years. Not resolved.

Gavin Lerena

Family chart This archive earlier recorded the South African jockey Gavin Lerena as a public figure and expressly claimed no relationship. The family's own descendant chart now places him: son of Carlos "Tex" Lerena, son of Ricardo, son of Pablo Armando — which would make him Cheryl's first cousin once removed, and David's second cousin.

Recorded as the family holds it, and not yet verified against a public record. It is, however, exactly what this archive predicted before any of it was known: that the sixty-one South African Lerenas are one kindred, and that a rare surname plus a shared trade is a strong signal.

Where it joins the other archives

Cheryl Anne Lerena married into the Defranceski family. That marriage is the hinge between this archive and the Defranceski archive, whose line runs back through Johannesburg to Gračišće and the Istrian peninsula. Their son married into the Falco line of Arienzo in Campania.

Three archives, three continents of origin, meeting in Brisbane: Lerena from the River Plate by way of South Africa, Defranceski from Istria, Falco from Campania.

What is still missing

The line is solid downward from Pablo Armando Lerena, and his own birth is fixed to a city and a day by his own passport — Rosario de Santa Fe, 22 February 1882. Above him there is one generation and no more. His enrolment card names Juan Carlos Lerena and María Lerena, and that is the whole of it: two names, given by their son, and not a single other record of either of them anywhere in Argentina or Uruguay.

And the obvious way to supply them has now been closed. The Rosario cathedral baptism index has been read letter by letter, under any surname, for 1881, 1882 and 1883 — twenty-six sections in each year, the cathedral being the city’s only parish until 1888 — and he is in none of it. The marriage registers are clear from 1868 to 1891. Uruguayan civil registration, which began in 1879, does not hold him either; nor do the indexed Spanish parish records. So the hope has moved off birth records altogether, onto three letters now with archives: his military enrolment in Buenos Aires, which would name his parents outright; the consular file behind his 1940 passport, which would show what he produced to prove he was Argentine; and the anarchist press in Amsterdam, which named the men deported from Argentina in the years he left.

The name above him, tested

The 1908 enrolment card names Pablo Armando's parents — Juan Carlos Lerena and Maria Lerena — and it is the only document in this archive that names them. On 17 September 2026 that name was put to every indexed record FamilySearch holds.

The control was run before the zeroes were written down. The same query form, with Gilberto Lerena and Julia Salvanach substituted, returns eleven records. The form works, so the zeroes below are the corpus and not the search.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT MEAN - IT MEANS: the man named on the card is absent from FamilySearch's INDEXED corpus, and so is the marriage that would name him. The route to Pablo Armando's parents does not run through the indexes, and further index searching is not where the next hour should go. IT DOES NOT MEAN he did not exist, and it does not exclude him from the records themselves. This tests the exact string 'Juan Carlos'. A record indexed as 'Juan C.', 'J. C.', or plain 'Juan' would not match - and the fuzzy form of this search returns 47, among them the children of a Juan Lerena and Eduviges Pelaez at Trinidad, Flores, whom this archive already holds. And it tests INDEXED records only. Unindexed images are a separate corpus with its own search.
WHERE THIS POINTS NEXT - Away from indexes and towards registers read by eye. The 1882 Rosario walk already proved the index complete across 750 consecutive acts, February to July, with no Lerena - so he was not baptised at the Catedral. Between them, those two results say the parents' generation is not in any indexed corpus this archive can reach, and the remaining routes are unindexed images, Argentine civil registration, and the other Rosario parishes.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.
