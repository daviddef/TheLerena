What we cannot close

Not the same page as the open questions. These are the places where the work has been done properly and the record may simply not exist — where more effort is not obviously the answer.

An archive that lists only its questions implies they are all answerable. Some are not. A parish register with a gap in it, a marriage in a country nobody has narrowed, a farm held on a lease that left no title — these do not yield to persistence.

Each row says what was actually done, so that nobody repeats it, and then says plainly what would have to exist for the gap to be closable at all.

Unfinished, and possibly unfinishable

Where Pablo Armando was baptised

What has been done
The Rosario cathedral index read letter by letter for 1881-1883; the whole L and Ll sections read end to end for 1879-1901; and the register ITSELF read act by act for February and March 1882 - 261 baptisms, every margin.

Why it may never close
Until 1888 the cathedral was the city's only parish, so there is nowhere else in Rosario he could be. Either he was baptised outside the city, not as an infant, or the register itself has a gap. If the act was never written, no amount of reading will find it.

Maria Lerena's maiden name

What has been done
The enrolment card writes her with her husband's surname. Rosario marriages read clean for 1867-1885 at full resolution. No Juan Carlos Lerena in any indexed record in Argentina or Uruguay.

Why it may never close
If they married in Italy or Spain before emigrating, the record is in a parish nobody has identified, in a country nobody has narrowed. The death notice may name her; if it does not, this may be the end.

Whether he was a Consul General

What has been done
The 1940 passport shows him as the BEARER; the consular signature is A. B. Bayne. Argentina had no legation in South Africa until 1950.

Why it may never close
An honorary consulship leaves a trace in the host state's gazette and almost nowhere else. If the exequatur was never gazetted, the tradition cannot be tested at all.

The eight years between Northam and the Cape

What has been done
Narrowed to two candidate ships. Passenger indexes searched under Taylor, Lerena, Lerina and Larena.

Why it may never close
If he came with the remounts rather than as a passenger, he is in military and shipping paperwork that was never indexed by name - and much of it was destroyed.

The ostrich farm

What has been done
The deeds registers show him holding no land at all.

Why it may never close
A leased farm leaves no deeds trace. Absence of a title is not absence of a farm, and there may be nothing to find either way.

The Rosario death index breaks off at F for 1840-1881

What has been done
The "Indice de defunciones 1800-1888" was opened and read under L on 13 September 2026. Its first sequence (1800-1839) and its second (1882-1888) both have L sections, both were read in full, and neither holds a Lerena. Its THIRD sequence, the one covering roughly 1840 to 1881, runs A, B, C, D, E, F and stops - the volume ends mid-alphabet.

Why it may never close
For this to be closable at all, an L section for 1840-1881 would have to exist somewhere - in another film, or in the parish's own holdings at Rosario. Failing that, the death REGISTERS for 1841-1875 survive and are filmed - VERIFIED IN THE CATALOGUE ON 13 SEPTEMBER 2026, and it is THREE films, not one: DGS 4531021 (1841-1860), 4531022 (1861-1864) and 4531023 (1864-1875). This archive had listed only the last of them. The first of the three was opened the same day: 658 images, unrestricted, and its leader card reads film 1091422, matching the catalogue exactly. So the route is real and precisely specified - it is simply long. The catalogue also confirms the hard edge at the other end: THERE IS NO DEFUNCIONES FILM AFTER 1875, so deaths 1876-1881 have neither an index section nor a register and cannot be reached at all. That is the only remaining route, and it is a very large one. For deaths after 1875 the collection holds no register at all.

The CEMLA manifests themselves

What has been done
CEMLA's public database has been searched under Lerena, Llerena, Larena and Lerina, and its index rows are recorded here in full. The underlying SHIP MANIFESTS - the pages the index was made from - have never been seen.

Why it may never close
CEMLA's search is behind a CAPTCHA this archive will not solve, and on the evidence of its own interface it returns index rows rather than page images. If that is right, the manifests are only at the Archivo General de la Nacion in Buenos Aires and this is not closable online by anyone. Recorded so that a future reader does not assume an index row is a document.

These are distinct from the open questions, which are answerable and merely unanswered, and from the hypotheses, which are propositions this archive is entertaining without having proved.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena

And for children: Our Family — The same ten families, told for a six-year-old.

An evidence-first family archive for the Lerena family — of Rosario and the River Plate, of the Cape and the Rand, and of Brisbane. Every claim carries its confidence. Living people are named — name and relationship only, never a date, a place, a photograph or a record.
