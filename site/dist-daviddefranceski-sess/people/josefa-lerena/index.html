Josefa LERENA (godmother, 1885)

A LERENA WOMAN THIS ARCHIVE HAS NEVER MET, found 14 September 2026. She stood godmother to Gilberto Lerena's son Raul at the Catedral Basilica de Montevideo on 10 September 1885, with Alfredo Costa. A woman of the surname at the font of a Lerena child is very likely one of the siblings of that generation - Gilberto had at least five - or a first cousin. Nothing else in this archive names her, and she is worth chasing. NOT A CORRECTION - A CROSS-REFERENCE THIS ROW LACKED, written 20 September 2026. "Nothing else in this archive names her" is still true: no second record names this godmother. But two rows here hold candidates for her and this one sent the reader off to chase somebody already being chased. JOSEFA LERENA TRAIBEL (1851-1947), daughter of Avelino, whose row has called itself a strong candidate for this godmother since 14 September; and "Lerena de L[erena], JOSEFA, 43, viuda", line 4 of Gilberto's 1895 Buenos Aires household, which the household file calls "almost certainly" this woman. AND THE TWO CANDIDATES FIGHT EACH OTHER, which no row here has said. The 1895 enumerator writes wives as [maiden surname] "de L." - Lenguas de Lerena, Salvanach de Lerena - so line 4 is a woman born a Lerena who married a Lerena. But Josefa Lerena Traibel married a MORATORIO, on a parish act, and would have been written "Lerena de Moratorio". So either the "de L." reading is wrong, or the 1895 widow and the Traibel daughter are two different women - and at most one of the two can be this godmother. Three rows, one name, and no act joining any two of them.

The other archives

The Lerena Archive is one of seven, each following a different line of the same family. They use the same method and the same five words for how sure a thing is.

  • The Defranceschi Archive Istria · Kvarner · Carnia A family written down under eight sovereigns, in four languages, and spelled at least nine different ways. Defranceschi · De Franceschi · Defranceski · Defrančeski
  • The Falco Archive Arienzo, near Naples Nine documented generations born, married and buried in one valley, and then a migrant ship to Brisbane. Falco · Zampiello · Morgillo · Crisci · Liguori · Maione · Rivetti · Annecchino
  • The Blažević Archive Senj and the Krmpotska mountains An evidence-first archive for the family of Hedviga Blažević of Senj — carriers, a tobacco factory, and a wall grave. Blažević · Papić · Vukelić · Žubrinić · Kalanj
  • The Booyzen Archive Graaff-Reinet · Cradock · Johannesburg A Cape Dutch family that kept marrying British women, changed one letter of its name in a war, and came off the train at Johannesburg with nothing. Booyzen · Booysen · Kolbe · Barry · Downing · van Jaarsveld · Schoeman
  • The D'Arcy Archive England and Ireland · Parramatta · Brisbane Out with the 39th Regiment in 1825, and four generations afterwards in two Brisbane cemeteries. D'Arcy · Saniger · Sneyd · Hornby · Wakefield · Atkinson · Murdoch
  • The Mazza Archive Sicily and Calabria · Brisbane Four Italian quarters — Mazza of Piedimonte Etneo, and Prostamo, Arena and Polistena of Calabria — ending in four graves in a row at Nudgee. Mazza · Prostamo · Arena · Polistena
