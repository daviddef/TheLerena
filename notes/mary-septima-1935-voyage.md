# Mary Septima's 1935 voyage — the best new lead in months

**Source:** findmypast index rows, screenshotted by David, 9 September 2026. **Not yet opened.** These are
index entries only; the images have not been ordered. Everything below is what the index says plus what
follows from it.

## The two rows

| Name | Born | Year | Record set | Place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lerena, Mary Septima** | 1885 | **1935** | Britain and Ireland, **Incoming** Passenger Lists 1878–1960 | Great Britain |
| **Lerena, M** | 1885 | **1935** | Passenger Lists **Leaving UK** 1890–1960 | **Cape, South Africa** |

## Why this matters

**Mary Septima Taylor** was born at **Bideford, Devon, on 17 September 1884** (GRO registration, in David's
own tree — the index year of 1885 is the usual off-by-one of an indexed age). She married Pablo Armando
Lerena. **She died on 15 November 1935.**

So these two rows say that **in the last year of her life she sailed to England and sailed back to the
Cape.** She was born in Devon and had lived in South Africa since at least 1905, when her eldest son Roque
was born at Cape Town. This looks very much like **a woman going home to England one last time.**

That is the human reading and it may be wrong — she may have gone for any reason. But the two rows
together establish something no other record has: **a dated, named, two-way voyage by a member of this
family**, in a year we can already fix from her death.

## *** Why the outward list is the one to order first ***

An **outward passenger list from the UK names everyone travelling in the party** — with ages, occupations,
last address in the UK, and intended destination. If Pablo Armando or any of the children travelled with
her, they are on that page.

That would give us, for the first time:
- **Pablo Armando's own hand-recorded age, occupation and address** on a British government form — a
  document type we have never had for him;
- possibly his **nationality as declared to a British official**, which bears directly on whether he was
  Argentine-born;
- a **ship and a date**, which anchors the family in time between the 1911 marriage and the 1950 estate.

The **incoming** list is worth having too — it gives her port of arrival, and the address she was heading
to in Britain, which may be **Taylor kin still in Devon**. Her line is already traced back to William
Taylor, coachman, of Northam and Bideford, and James Taylor b. c.1796.

## What to do
1. Open **Lerena, M — Passenger Lists Leaving UK 1890–1960, 1935, Cape South Africa**. Note every other
   passenger on the page, not only the Lerenas.
2. Open **Lerena, Mary Septima — Britain and Ireland Incoming Passenger Lists, 1935**. Note the UK address.
3. Check the **dates against 15 November 1935**. If the return sailing is close to her death, that is worth
   knowing plainly, and worth handling gently on the site.

## Two other findmypast rows worth pulling

**Lerena, Juanita Ramona, b. 1937 — arriving Great Britain 1957.** Roque Luis Armando married Rosalina
Wilhelmina Chappell in September 1936, and his daughter appears in the 1973 estate papers as
*Juanita Ramond Hutson (born Lerena)*. **A Juanita Ramona Lerena born 1937 fits exactly.** Probably ours.

**A family group of four arriving in Great Britain in 1958** — *Carlos Gustavo* b.1915, *Nelly Maria
Delvalle* b.1922, *Carlos Gilberto* b.1946, *Eleonora* b.1947. Not ours on present evidence, but
***Carlos Gilberto*** carries **both** of the names that define the Argentine Stud Book family, where
Gilberto Lerena (b.1854, Uruguay) named a son **Carlos** Augusto Federico (b.1883). The pairing is not a
coincidence of common names — Gilberto is rare. This is the Gilberto thread surfacing a third time, after
the 1895 Buenos Aires census and the 1928 CEMLA arrival.

## One cross-confirmation, recorded because it validates the method
findmypast has **Avelino Carlos Lerena, 1940, United States passenger and crew lists**. CEMLA has
**Avelino Carlos Lerena, Argentine, arriving Buenos Aires from NEW YORK on 22 January 1940**. Two
independent indexes, two ends of the same journey. Neither is ours, but it shows the surname cohort is
being reconstructed accurately.
