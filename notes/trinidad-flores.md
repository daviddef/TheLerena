# Trinidad, Flores — the nest of the surname

**Source:** findmypast, *Uruguay Baptisms 1750–1900*, surname LERENA, read 9 September 2026 from the free
result list. Transcripts are paywalled and were not opened; what follows uses only the indexed fields —
child, birth year, baptism year, **father, mother** and place.

## The finding

Thirty-three index rows. Nineteen distinct children. And they are not scattered — **eleven of them are
baptised in one small town: Trinidad, in the department of Flores, Uruguay**, all within the 1870s and
early 1880s, to **six different fathers**:

| Father | Mother | Children |
|---|---|---|
| **Bernabé** | Casimira | Paula Modesta 1875 · Carmen 1877 · Ysidro Gregorio 1885 |
| **Vitalino** | Andrea | Juana Alejandra 1873 · Gervasio 1875 · Rosario Fulgencia 1877 |
| **Ysaac** | Adelaida | Alejandra Raymunda 1874 · Maria Luisa Lina 1875 |
| **Cipriano** | Lauriana | Doroteo Donato 1875 · Maria Leonor 1877 |
| **Fermín** | Paulina | Faustino 1877 |
| **Luis** | Julia | Emma Liseta 1876 |

Six men of the same rare surname, fathering children in the same small town in the same handful of years,
are **overwhelmingly likely to be brothers or first cousins**. This looks like the founding kindred itself.

A second, separate group sits in **Montevideo**: Gilberto & Julia (Carlos Augusto Federico, 1883),
Manuel & Dolores (Juan Pascual, 1884), Manuel & Juana (Felicia, 1891), Pedro A. & Manuela (Manuel Antonio,
1883). And a third at **San José de Mayo**: Mauricio & Casimira (Ysidoro Belisario, 1861).

## Why this matters to us

1. ***It independently confirms the Gilberto family.*** *Carlos Augusto Federico Lerena, born 1883 at
   Montevideo, father **Gilberto**, mother **Julia*** — exactly the "Carlos A. Lerena, 11, born Uruguay"
   found in the **1895 Argentine national census** in the Buenos Aires household of **Gilberto Lerena**,
   with **Julia Salvanach** as mother. Two unrelated sources, the same family. The Gilberto who co-founded
   the **Argentine Stud Book** was a Uruguayan from this stock who moved to Buenos Aires between 1887 and
   1889 — the census birthplaces switch from Uruguay to Buenos Aires in exactly those years.

2. ***It explains the Forebears distribution.*** Uruguay has the highest density of the surname in the
   world, about 1 in 7,962 people. A single founding cluster in one small inland town is precisely the
   signature that produces that number.

3. ***It gives the surname a home to search.*** Until now "Uruguay" was a hypothesis. It is now a town:
   **Trinidad, Flores** — and a set of forenames that repeat: Bernabé, Vitalino, Ysaac, Cipriano, Fermín,
   Luis, Mauricio, Manuel, Pedro, Gilberto.

## And the negative, stated plainly

***There is no Pablo and no Armando anywhere in this set.*** Nineteen children, none of them ours.

That is worth something, but not too much. This index covers selected parishes, not all of Uruguay, and it
stops at **1900** — so it would hold a birth in 1882, but only if the parish was one of the indexed ones.
Trinidad, Montevideo and San José de Mayo are covered; most of the country is not. **It weakens the
Uruguayan-birth idea without killing it.**

## What to do next
1. **Uruguayan civil registration began in 1879**, three years before Pablo Armando's stated birth — far
   earlier than Santa Fe's. If he was born in Uruguay in 1882 there should be a **civil birth record**, and
   those are held by the *Dirección General del Registro de Estado Civil*. This is now a much sharper
   target than a parish-by-parish trawk.
2. **Trace the Trinidad kindred forward.** If one of those six brothers had a son in 1882 who is not in
   this index, he is the candidate.
3. **Follow Gilberto.** He is the only Lerena we can place in the right country, the right decade and the
   right trade, and he now has a documented Uruguayan origin, a wife (Julia Salvanach) and children.
