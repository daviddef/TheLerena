# The Uruguayan turn

*13 September 2026. One name in a hundred and seventy years of parish index — and when the act behind it
was opened, it said the family was not Argentine.*

## The act

**Rosario, Nuestra Señora del Rosario (Catedral). *Bautismos 1865–1867*, image 124 of 311, folio 229,
act 1541.** Image ark `3:1:S3HT-XS9C-ZL`. Handwritten, read at full tile resolution in four overlapping
renders. The hand is Pbro. Pantaleón Gallo's; his signature closes the page.

> *(1541º) En el día diez y siete de Febrero de mil ochocientos sesenta y seis, el in-*
> *frascripto, bauticé solemnemente á una criatura que se le puso por nombres*
> ***María, Julia, Margarita, Josefa, Emirena y Justiniana***, *que nació el ocho de*
> *Octubre del año próximo pasado.* **Es hija legítima de Don Luis Lerena Len-**
> **guas y de Doña Julia Juanicó, naturales del E. Oriental;** *fueron padrinos*
> *Don Luis Lerena, representado por don Claudino Campos y Doña Margarita*
> *Mara de Carlos. Y por verdad lo firmo yó el Cura Encargado.*

Born **8 October 1865**. Baptised **17 February 1866**.

## What it says

Three things, and the third is the one that matters.

**One.** The compound surname in the 1945 index is real. The child is a **Lerena Lenguas** because her
father is: **Don Luis Lerena Lenguas**.

**Two.** Her mother is **Doña Julia Juanicó** — and Juanicó is not an Argentine name in this period. It
is a **Montevideo** name.

**Three, and plainly stated in the act itself:** ***naturales del E. Oriental.*** Both parents are
**natives of the Estado Oriental del Uruguay.**

There is a fourth thing, softer but of a piece. The godfather is another **Don Luis Lerena**, and he did
not attend — he was **represented by proxy** by Don Claudino Campos. Godparents stand by proxy when they
are somewhere else. The obvious somewhere else, for a Uruguayan family with a Uruguayan godfather, is
**Montevideo**.

## Why this is the most useful thing found this week

Here is the position before this act was read.

Pablo Armando Lerena was born at Rosario on **22 February 1882**. His own army enrolment card names his
parents: **Juan Carlos Lerena and María Lerena**. But the Rosario baptism index — **both volumes, 1731
to 1901, now read end to end** — contains **no baptism for him**, and contains the surname Lerena
**once** in a hundred and seventy years. The marriage index carries no Lerena either. A family that
lived in a parish for a generation leaves a trail of baptisms; this one leaves nothing.

That was a hole. It now has a shape.

**The Lerenas of the Río de la Plata are a Montevideo family.** The single Lerena who turns up in
Rosario in the whole of the nineteenth century is a Uruguayan who was passing through, marrying into
another Montevideo house, and standing his own father as godfather by proxy across the river. If the
surname belongs to the eastern bank, then **Juan Carlos Lerena came to Rosario from somewhere else** —
and the first place to look is Uruguay.

Which would explain, in one stroke, every negative this archive has collected:

- no Rosario baptism for Pablo Armando — because the family was not a parish family;
- no Rosario baptism for Juan Carlos — because he was not born there;
- no Lerena marriage at Rosario — because it happened elsewhere;
- no Lerena in the Rosario index at all, but for one Uruguayan.

## And what it does not say

It does **not** say Luis Lerena Lenguas is related to Juan Carlos Lerena. Two men sharing a surname in
the same city sixteen years apart is a lead, not a link. **She is not Pablo Armando's sister**, and no
line has been drawn between these households.

It does not say Pablo Armando was Uruguayan. He was born at Rosario; that is documented twice over.

And it sits *awkwardly* beside the DNA. Cheryl Lerena's MyHeritage results put her in a **Río de la
Plata** genetic group — which is precisely the ambiguity here, because the Río de la Plata is **both
banks**. The DNA cannot tell Buenos Aires from Montevideo. It never could. What it does say is that the
Argentine-side hypothesis and the Uruguayan-side hypothesis are, genetically, the same hypothesis.

## What to do next, in order

1. **The Montevideo cathedral registers.** FamilySearch holds *Uruguay, Registros Parroquiales,
   1750–1930*. Look for **Juan Carlos Lerena** — a baptism in the 1840s or 1850s, and a marriage to a
   **María** before 1882.
2. **Luis Lerena Lenguas and Julia Juanicó**, in Montevideo — their marriage, and his own baptism. If his
   father is the *Don Luis Lerena* who stood godfather by proxy, that is two generations in one act.
3. **The Uruguayan civil registers** from 1879, and the Montevideo *padrón*.
4. **Reconsider the excluded LERINA entries.** Four Italian arrivals of 1889 were excluded on the
   reasoning that *Lerina* is an Italian surname and not a variant of the Spanish *Lerena*. That
   reasoning is untouched by this find — but it was reached the same way the Armando Lorena card was
   reached, and that one was wrong. It is on the list.

## The honest caveat about the surname

*Mara de Carlos*, the godmother's surname, is not securely read. Everything else on the page is. Where
this note makes a claim it is because the words are legible at level 13, not because they fit.
