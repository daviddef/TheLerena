# The anarchist press, answered

*10 September 2026. The letter to Amsterdam of 9 September has been answered, and the answer is a null
with a usable shape.*

## What the IISH said

The International Institute of Social History — the world's principal repository for the international
left — replied through `ask@iisg.nl`:

- Their Latin American periodicals, **Argentinian included**, are **digitised and OCR'd**, and searchable
  as *Latin American Anarchist and Labour Periodicals Online, c. 1880–1940* at
  `degruyterbrill.com/database/lalp/start`.
- **"I have done a quick search on the name Lerena and its spelling variations, however nothing popped
  up so far."**
- Their other Argentine holdings sit on different themes and periods.
- They cannot research for us, but will **scan specific items at €0.50 each** (`repro@iisg.nl`).

## Re-run here, and what the noise actually is

The archivist's *"nothing popped up"* is right in substance but loose in wording: the database
**does** return hits. Knowing what they are is worth more than the word "nothing", because it tells the
next person what to ignore.

| Search | Hits | What they actually are |
|---|---|---|
| **Lerena** | **171** | Almost entirely **"Conde de Lerena"** — Pedro López de Lerena, Charles III's finance minister, whose 1787 *Censo* is cited in Spanish economic history; and **"Osimani y Llerena"**, a *street in Montevideo* where the anarchist weekly *La Tierra* had its office. |
| **Lerina** | **16** | OCR breakage of **"ballerina"** — *"una bal lerina"*, *"La Contessa bal-lerina"*. Nothing else. |

> **No person of this family appears in the Latin American anarchist and labour press, 1880–1940.**

## What that does to the family tradition

David's mother told it again the night before this archive opened: *Armando ran away from Argentina
because he was a communist on the run.* The tradition now has **three things against it**:

1. **He is not in the left press**, on the corpus best placed to hold him, searched by its own archivist
   and again here.
2. **In 1908 he walked into his own government's consular office at Cape Town** and wrote down his name,
   his conscript class, his birthplace and both his parents. A man hiding from the Argentine state does
   not do that.
3. **Ley 4144 deported foreigners, not nationals.** He was Argentine by birth — the mechanism the story
   implies could not have been used on him.

## And the honest weight of it

**The first of those three is much the weakest, and it should not be dressed up.** A horse-dealer in his
twenties would not appear in the anarchist press *unless he were notable* — most people never appear in
any periodical at all. Absence from a newspaper corpus is close to no evidence about an ordinary man.

**The second is the real one.** The enrolment card is a positive act, in his own hand's presence, at the
moment the story says he was in flight.

So: **not disproved, and now unlikely.** The archive keeps the tradition on the hypotheses page, moved
down, with the reasons stated.

## What is still worth spending money on

The €0.50 scans matter only if a target is identified first, and none is. **The AGN Ley 4144 expulsion
files remain the only place that could settle this positively** — and that request needs David to
register with a passport.
