## What was deliberately *not* done

The Source Linker offers to copy the indexed values into the tree. **They were refused.** Accepting them
would have written *Rosario de Lerma, Salta* over his birthplace and created a father called
*Juan Carlos Lorena Serena*. The parents were created **by hand from the image** — *Lerena*, not
*Lorena*; no *Serena* — because *Serena* is not a name at all but the printed word **SERIE** on the line
below the parents.

## What the reason statements say

Every FamilySearch edit carries a reason statement, and these were written for a stranger who will one
day find the profile and wonder why the tree disagrees with the index.

**On the card itself**, attached to Pablo Armando: the four ways the computer index differs from the
image; the three independent confirmations (the 1882 class matching his passport, the Cape Town
enrolling office matching his life and death at the Cape, the passport's own *Guardia Nacional 6º de
Caballería 1915*); and the son named *Ricardo Juan Carlos*. It ends: **"Please read the image before
correcting the indexed fields."**

**On María**, in capitals so it cannot be skimmed past:

> **CAUTION, PLEASE DO NOT TREAT LERENA AS PROVEN TO BE HER MAIDEN NAME:** she is written with her
> husband's surname… The card cannot decide it, and no other record of her has been found.

The doubt travels with the finding. It would have been easy to publish two clean names and let the tree
harden around them; that is how bad genealogy spreads, and this archive has spent a week undoing exactly
that kind of confident error in other people's work — and in its own.

## Why publish at all

Before today the profile said only **ADD PARENT**. Whoever built that branch knew he was a *horse dealer
and trainer* also known as *Robert Paul* — a relative, or someone close to the family — and still could
not get above him. The card had been sitting behind a machine index that spelled him *Armand Lorena* and
put him in Salta. It is now attached where the next person to look will find it.
