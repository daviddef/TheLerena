# The death that was hiding under a misspelling

*13 September 2026.*

## What was open

Luis Eugenio Lerena Lenguas — father of the one Lerena baptised at Rosario before 1879 — had a
death somewhere between **July 1886** (he fathered a child baptised then, act 1720) and **11 July
1910** (Julia died a widow). Twenty-four years of window.

A 1,502-row sweep of Montevideo Lerena deaths for 1880–1911 was run that morning and did not
contain him. A narrower sweep of *Luis* Lerena deaths for 1899–1903 returned 132 rows and did not
contain him either. Both were reported here as negatives, and the FamilySearch Tree's assertion of
"1840–1901" was written up as a lead that the registers appeared to contradict.

**The registers did not contradict it. The index did.**

## How it was found

By searching for his **mother**.

Every search until then had gone through the surname, and the surname is exactly what breaks. The
Montevideo clerks and the FamilySearch indexers between them have written this family as **Lerena,
Lorena, Serena, Languas, Lenquas, Loupuan** and **Guanico**. A search on *Justiniana Lenguas* as
mother — a rarer, more stable name — returned eight rows, and one of them was:

> *Luis **Serena** Lenguas · Death Registration 24 December 1901 · Montevideo · father Luis Serena,
> mother Justiniana Lenguas · **spouse Julia Guanico***

That is him, under an S.

## The act

Montevideo, 14th section, certificate **No. 216**, registrar Antonio F. Mula. Image read at
DeepZoom level 13. The margin of the register itself reads **LERENA LENGUAS, LUIS** — the family
did not use "Serena"; a transcriber did.

> *…y en la casa calle **Suarez Nº 25** ha fallecido **Luis Lerena Lenguas** — de nacionalidad
> **oriental** — nacido en **Montevideo** — de **cincuenta y seis** de edad — de estado **casado** —
> y de profesión **hacendado** — á consecuencia de **Tuberculosis Pulmonar**, según consta del
> certificado del Dr. Scremini… Que el finado era hijo legítimo de D. **Luis Lerena**, oriental,
> [fallecido], y de Da. **Justiniana Lenguas**, oriental, de **setenta y nueve años**, de profesión
> labores, domiciliada accidentalmente en la calle Suarez Nº 25.*

Four things it gives that nothing else did:

1. **A date and an address.** 24 December 1901, at seven in the morning, at calle Suárez 25 in el
   **Paso del Molino**.
2. **A profession: *hacendado*** — a landowner. Until now the best this archive had on the family's
   standing was the Rosario act of 1866 calling them *naturales del E. Oriental*. This says what he
   was, and it is not nothing: a *hacendado* holds land.
3. **His mother was alive, at 79, in the house.** Justiniana Lenguas — born about **1822** —
   outlived her son and was staying at the address where he died. His father was already dead.
4. **"De estado casado."** He died married, which is precisely how Julia comes to be a widow at her
   own death nine years later. The two acts now lock together.

## The lesson, and it is not a new one

This archive already knows that an index is a finding aid and the act is the evidence — see
[[lerena-evidence-discipline]]. What today added is the other half: **when a search fails, suspect
the index before you conclude the record does not exist.** Two negative sweeps were run and written
up honestly, and both were right about what they searched and wrong about what they implied.

The working rule now: **for a family whose surname is unstable, search on the rarest stable name in
the household.** Here that was the mother's. It did not only produce the death — the same eight
rows produced four of Luis Eugenio's siblings, and put **Gilberto Lerena** among them. See
[[the-racing-dynasty-joins-the-trunk]].
