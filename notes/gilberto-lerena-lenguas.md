# Gilberto Lerena Lenguas — the one Lerena in print

*10 September 2026. Chased because he is the only bearer of this surname documented in books rather than
only in registers, and because the family remembered horses.*

## The man

**Gilberto Justiniano LERENA LENGUAS**, born **24 May 1855**, died **8 December 1912**.
Married **Julia Salvañach**.

- Entered the turf in **1887**, founding his **Stud Oriental**.
- **Promoter, founder and Secretary of the Stud Book Argentino**, whose first act he signed on
  **11 June 1893**. (The Stud Book itself was created by the Jockey Club on 30 June 1882 — the year
  Pablo Armando was born, which is a coincidence and is recorded as one.)
- Bred **Old Man**, winner of the Argentine **Quadruple Crown**.
- Associated with **Stud Oriental, Stud Bend Or, Haras La Guardia, Haras El Moro, Haras Las Ortigas**,
  and his own **Haras El Viejo**.
- In **1914** the *Premio Abril* at Palermo — run since 1905 — was renamed the
  **Gran Premio Gilberto Lerena** in his honour. It is still run, as a **Group 1** over 2,200 m.

## The family behind him

**Lerena Lenguas is a Uruguayan name, and a rich one.** *Luis Lerena Lenguas* owned a country house of
fifteen rooms and four galleries at what is now **Juanicó, Canelones**, photographed for the **1889 Paris
exhibition** under the title *"Château de Mr. Louis Lerena Lenguas."* **Josefina Lerena Acevedo de
Blixen** (1889–1967), the writer, belongs to the same connection.

## Where he touches this archive

He has been on the register for days from two independent directions, and today a third:

1. **The 1895 Argentine census** — *Gilberto Lerena, 41, born 1854 in Uruguay, married*, head of a
   household in Buenos Aires. The dates match.
2. **Cajón 985** — the army enrolment drawer that holds Pablo Armando's card. **Gilberto's sons are five
   cards away from him**, indexed under four different spellings — *Lorena*, *Lerena*, *Perena* — with
   their mother given every time as *Julia Salvañach*. Their birthplaces: Federal (Entre Ríos), Uruguay,
   Montecarlo (Misiones), Montevideo.
3. **The turf press**, which is where he is a public figure rather than an index entry.

## And what it comes to

**Nothing that connects him to Pablo Armando.** No shared parent, no shared place, no Rosario. His
family is Uruguayan and moneyed; ours is a horse-dealer at the Cape whose father cannot be found in any
register on either side of the river.

**The temptation here is obvious and is being refused.** A rare surname, a famous horseman, a family
that remembered horses, and a great-grandson who trained racehorses at the Cape — it would be very easy
to write a paragraph implying a link. There is no evidence of one, and this archive does not publish
implications.

What the search *does* give is a **date-limited fact**: the Lerena name was, in Gilberto's generation,
attached to **Uruguayan money and Argentine bloodstock**. If Pablo Armando's people were connected to
that world at any remove, the connection would be through **Uruguay**, not Rosario — and the card says
Rosario. Recorded as tension, not resolved.

## Two doors that would not open

- **geni.com** — has a profile for him with parents and siblings. It sits behind an hCaptcha, which this
  archive does not solve. **A researcher with a browser can read it in a minute**, and it would answer
  the only question worth asking of him: *was there a Juan Carlos in that family?*
- **Geneanet** (`gw.geneanet.org/gentuc`, tree of Ernesto Álvarez Uriondo) — has *Gilberto Lerena
  Salvañach*. Behind Cloudflare's bot check. Same answer, same one-minute job for a person.

Both are listed rather than quietly dropped, because they are the next step and it is a small one.
