# Uruguayan civil registration, 1882 — the test, and the result

**Run 9 September 2026** against FamilySearch's indexed collections, chiefly
***Uruguay, Civil Registration, 1879–2020***, with *Uruguay, Catholic Church Records 1726–2000*,
*Uruguay Baptisms 1750–1900* and the *Uruguay Civil Registration Index Cards 1900–1937* alongside.

## Why this was the next test

The Rosario baptism sweep is finished and exhausted: 1881, 1882 and 1883, all twenty-six letter sections,
no Pablo Armando. Three things then pointed at Uruguay rather than at another Argentine parish:

1. **Uruguay has the highest density of this surname in the world** — about 1 in 7,962 people.
2. **The kindred has a home**: eleven Lerena children baptised at **Trinidad, in the department of Flores**,
   in the 1870s, to six different fathers.
3. ***Uruguayan civil registration began in 1879*** — three years before his stated birth, and **seven
   years before Santa Fe's**. That is the whole reason the Argentine trail dead-ends, and it does not apply
   here.

If he was Uruguayan-born in 1882, there should be a civil birth record.

## The result

***There is no Pablo Armando Lerena in the Uruguayan records at all.***

Every **Pablo Lerena** the index holds for Uruguay, in full:

| Name | Event | Place | Parents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pablo Granero **Llerena** | b. 9 Mar 1870 | Cordón, Montevideo | Roque Vicente Granero, Rosa María Llerena |
| **Pablo Evaristo Lerena Harrison** | m. 1902 | **Flores** | Pio C. Lerena, Bernardina Harrison |
| Pedro Pablo Lerena | b. 6 Aug 1910 | Montevideo | Pedro Segundo Lerena, Paulina Grande |
| Regino Pablo Olivera Lerena | b. 14 Jan 1912 | Flores | Bienaventura Olivera, Luisa Lerena |

**Not one born in 1882. Not one named Armando.** Nor does a *Pablo Armando* of any surname appear born in
Uruguay in 1882 — the nearest are **Pablo Armando Gimenez** (b. 15 Jan 1885, Soriano) and **Pablo Armando
Pilón** (b. 1885, Dolores, Soriano), both three years late and neither a Lerena.

## The control that makes this negative worth something

A null is only as good as the coverage behind it. **The index does hold Lerena births from exactly this
window** — *María Luisa Lerena, born 19 July 1882, Sección 1, Río Negro*; *Ema Justina Felicia Lerena,
b. 18 May 1881, San José*; *Alberto Julián Lerena, b. 17 Aug 1883, San José*; *Carlos Augusto Federico
Lerena, b. 9 July 1883, Montevideo*; *Manuel Antonio Lerena, b. 31 March 1883, Montevideo*. **1882 Lerena
births are indexed.** So this is not a hole in the coverage; it is an absence in the record.

**But it is an index, not the registers themselves.** FamilySearch's Uruguayan indexing is not complete for
every department and year, and an unindexed 1882 birth in a thinly-covered department would not surface
here. So the honest statement is: **he is not in the indexed Uruguayan civil registration**, which
substantially weakens the Uruguayan-birth idea without finally killing it.

## What it did give us — the Uruguayan kindred, much enlarged

The searches returned a workable genealogy of the Uruguayan Lerenas, and it clusters exactly where the
baptisms said it would.

**At Trinidad and elsewhere in Flores:** Bernabé Lerena m. Casimira Peláez (also recorded as Casimira de
Gutiérrez) · **Pio C. Lerena m. Bernardina Harrison** · Custodio Lerena m. Ema Cardozo · Emilio Lerena m.
Paula Casares · Venancio Alejandro Lerena m. Venancia · Luisa Lerena · and ***Gregorio ARMANDO Lerena***,
marrying at Flores in 1930, son of María Luisa Lerena — **the only Armando Lerena anywhere in these
records.**

**At Montevideo:** Gilberto Lerena m. Julia Salvañach · Manuel Lerena m. Dolores Espíndola · Pedro A.
Lerena m. Manuela Lemos, and also with Trinidad Morales · Pedro Segundo Lerena m. Paulina Grande.

**At San José:** Alberto Lerena m. María Inés Larriera · Arturo Lerena m. Ema Regules.

## Where this leaves things
Three of the four candidate birthplaces have now been tested and failed: **Rosario cathedral** (three years,
every letter), **Argentine baptisms generally** (two Lerena entries in the whole indexed country), and now
**Uruguayan civil registration**. What has *not* been tested is **Spain** — where the surname is most
numerous in absolute terms, concentrated on **Logroño, La Rioja**, exactly as the CEMLA arrivals showed —
and the possibility that he was baptised under a name we would not recognise.
