# Two brothers, a mother and her daughter

*10 September 2026. A correction to this archive's own record, and a family arrangement that reads
strangely until you put the dates in order.*

## What this archive had

`gen1-children.tsv` recorded Roque Luis Armando Lerena as marrying **"Rosalina Wilhelmina CHAPPELL"** on
21 September 1936, taken from the marriage index, which gives her as *Rosaline Wilhermina Forbes
Chappell*. Ricardo Juan Carlos's wife was recorded only as **"Doreen ?"**, from the family chart.

Both entries were wrong in the same way, and the same document fixes them.

## What is actually the case

**Roseline Wilhelmina FORBES** married **Frederick Christian Chappell** on **1 November 1920 at
Woodstock, Cape Town**. That marriage is in the South African civil marriage records *and* in the
Anglican parish registers of the Church of the Province of South Africa — two independent entries for
one event — and Frederick Christian Chappell has his own Western Cape estate file naming his wife as
*Rose Forbes Chappell*.

So:

- **CHAPPELL was her married name, not her maiden name.** Her maiden name was **FORBES**. The 1936
  register writes her *Rosaline Wilhermina Forbes Chappell* because that is exactly what a widow
  remarrying would be called.
- Frederick Christian Chappell died in **1933**.
- Three years later, in **September 1936**, his widow married **Roque Luis Armando Lerena**. She died
  **2 July 1950 at Johannesburg**, four months after her father-in-law Pablo Armando.
- Her eldest daughter by Chappell, **Doreen May Chappell, born 1921 at Cape Town**, married Roque's
  brother **Ricardo Juan Carlos Lerena**.

> **Two Lerena brothers married a mother and her daughter.** Ricardo's wife was his brother's
> stepdaughter; Roque's wife was his brother's mother-in-law.

Doreen's siblings — Roseline Evelyn (b.1925), Frederick John (1927–2003) and Edward Reginald Chappell —
became Roque's stepchildren in 1936.

## Where it comes from, and how far to trust it

The relationships come from the **FamilySearch Family Tree** (Ricardo `GPW4-5S5`, Doreen `GL8L-57C`,
Roseline `GL8L-BD4`, Frederick `GL8L-F5J`) — which is user-contributed and, on its own, not evidence.
**So it was checked before being written down**, and the check holds: the 1920 marriage exists twice
over in the record, in two different denominational series, with both names spelled as the tree gives
them; the estate file exists; the 1936 Lerena marriage names her exactly as a widow would be named; and
her 1950 death is registered as *Rosaline Wilhelmina Forbes Lerena*, b.1903.

**What is not verified** is the link itself — that *this* Doreen May Chappell is *that* Doreen. It rests
on the tree plus the coincidence of the surname in both marriages, which is strong but not a document.
Marked accordingly.

## A discrepancy left standing

Doreen's death is given as **17 November 1993** in the tree and in the Johannesburg cemetery register
(which agrees exactly on day and month, 17 November died, 20 November buried, but whose year FamilySearch
has mangled to "0099"). Yet this archive's own Government Gazette sweep found the estate of
**Doreen May Lerena advertised on 23 February 2001**. An estate can be advertised late, but six years is
a lot. **Either the year is 1999 and the tree is wrong, or the estate sat unadvertised.** Not resolved;
recorded.

## Why it matters beyond curiosity

Three of Roque and Roseline's children died as infants in the Transvaal — **Rieta Maria** (b.1936,
d. 28 August 1937), a **Ricardo Juan Carlos** named for his uncle and his Argentine grandfather, and
**Antoinette Septima Lynette** (born and died January 1943), who carried her grandmother **Mary
Septima**'s name. The naming discipline in this family is consistent and deliberate: the grandfather's
name and the grandmother's name are given again and again.

That is exactly the pattern that made **Ricardo Juan Carlos** — named, we now know, for
**Juan Carlos Lerena** — worth trusting as corroboration of the enrolment card. See
[the Cape Town enrolment card](the-cape-town-enrolment-card.md).
