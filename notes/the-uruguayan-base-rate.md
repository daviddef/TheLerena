# The Uruguayan base rate

*13 September 2026, evening. After the Rosario act pointed across the river, the same question was put
to every Lerena father already sitting in this archive's files — and the answer came back the same way
four times.*

## The drawer

Pablo Armando's 1908 enrolment card lives in army drawer **"Cajón 985 (Lera)"**. On 10 September this
archive read images 650–688 of that drawer, because the cards are filed **by sound** — Lera, Lerena,
Lorena, Serena, Sera, Era, Leza, Perena, Perella and Hereijo all interfiled — and so the Lerena section
is a near-complete roll of Argentine Lerena men of enrolment age in the 1910s–30s. **Each card names its
holder's father and mother.**

At the time that was used for one thing: to confirm that no card in the drawer but Pablo Armando's names
a *Juan Carlos* and a *María*. It still doesn't. But the fathers themselves were never followed up.

## The four fathers

| The father on the cards | His wife | Where **he** was born |
|---|---|---|
| **Gilberto Lerena** | Julia Salvanach | **Montevideo, 24 May 1855** — Gilberto Justiniano Lerena Lenguas |
| **Alejandro M. Lerena** | Julia Bassani | **Uruguay, 1839** — a son born at **Montevideo**, 1886 |
| **Arturo Lerena** | Amalia Díaz | **Montevideo, 1858** — son of Avelino Lerena and Ambrosia **Traibel** |
| **Nemesio Ramón Lerena** | Laura Escobite | married at **Salto, Uruguay**, 16 January 1903 — then Concordia, Entre Ríos, directly across the river |

Four branches. Four different provinces of settlement — **Entre Ríos, Mendoza, Misiones, Buenos Aires**
— and **one common origin, across the river**. Three more fathers in the drawer (Carlos Lerena of
Mendoza, Leandro Lorena, and a Concepción Lerena named as a mother) could not be traced either way;
Carlos named a son **Avelino**, which is a Lerena Lenguas and Lerena-Traibel given name, and that is
suggestive and nothing more.

## What it is, and what it is not

**It is not a link.** None of these men is Juan Carlos Lerena. None is shown to be related to him. The
archive has not moved one inch closer to a document naming his parents.

**What it is, is a base rate.** In Pablo Armando's generation, an Argentine Lerena is the son of a
Uruguayan family far more often than not — in every traceable case in the one drawer where they are
gathered together. Set beside the parish evidence, that matters:

- the Rosario baptism index holds **no Lerena** across both volumes but for one child, and **her parents
  were Uruguayan**;
- the Rosario marriage index, 1867–1885, holds **no Lerena** at all;
- the 1895 Argentine national census records **not one Lerena in Santa Fe**;
- and every Lerena in the enrolment drawer whose origin can be traced **came from Uruguay**.

**Juan Carlos Lerena, who left no trace whatever in the province where his son was born, fits that
shape exactly.** That is an argument about likelihood, not about descent, and it is filed as one.

## And the negative, which is worth as much

The indexed Uruguayan record was searched the same evening for the man himself. **There is no Juan
Carlos Lerena** born in Uruguay between 1830 and 1870 in *Uruguay, Catholic Church Records 1726–2000*,
in *Uruguay, Civil Registration 1879–2020*, or in the *Uruguay Baptisms 1750–1900* index; and no Lerena
groom marrying a **María** in Uruguay between 1865 and 1885. Every *Juan Carlos Lerena* the index does
return is a twentieth-century Uruguayan civil-registration card, decades too late.

So the record does not hand him over. What it does is narrow where the un-indexed pages worth turning
are — **Montevideo parish registers, image by image**, the way the Rosario index was read.

## One new name, recorded and not claimed

> **Armando Lerena**, aged 26, married, entry 28 on a **Buenos Aires → Montevideo** crossing of
> January or February 1910. The clerk wrote his nationality in the manifest in his own hand:
> ***Oriental***.

Read from the image, at tile level 13, rather than from the index — the house rule. He is **the only
other Armando Lerena found anywhere in the River Plate**, and *Armando* is not a common given name on
these lists.

**He is not Pablo Armando.** The nationality is wrong; Pablo Armando enrolled with the *Argentine* army
in 1908 and carried an Argentine passport in 1940. The age is two years out. And in 1910 Pablo Armando
was at the Cape, unmarried — he and Mary Septima did not marry until September 1911, six years after
their first son was born.

But an **Armando Lerena, Uruguayan, of almost exactly his age** is the shape a brother or a first cousin
would have. He goes on the register as **unplaced**, which is what the evidence supports and no more.
