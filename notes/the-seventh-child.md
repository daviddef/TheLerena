# The seventh child, and the act that named four grandparents

*13 September 2026.*

## What happened

The Montevideo household was opened earlier the same day: the marriage of **Luis Eugenio Lerena
Lenguas** and **Julia Juanicó** at San Francisco de Asís on 19 December 1864, act 258, and Julia's
death as a widow on 11 July 1910. Five children were assembled from primary records and written up.

Five was wrong. The five had been built outward from the 1910 death certificate, and **a death
certificate names the children who mattered to whoever registered the death — not the children a
woman bore.** Searching *both parents' names in one query* instead produced two more:

- **José Julio Lerena**, b. **15 March 1879**, Montevideo. Birth registered only on 14 September
  1908, when he was 29 — the late registration a man makes to produce proof of birth for his own
  wedding, and he does appear on a Montevideo marriage index card for 1908. Index-level; the image
  is not yet opened.
- **Cándido Jaime Lerena**, b. **13 September 1884**, baptised 6 July 1886. Image read.

## Act No. 1720, and what it gives

San Francisco de Asís, folio 470, act **1720**, in the hand of **Martín Pérez**. Read by the
DeepZoom tile method at level 12 — the maximum for this film.

> *En seis de Julio de mil ocho cientos ochenta y seis… bauticé solemnemente a un párvulo que puse
> por nombre **Cándido Jaime**, que nació el trece de Setiembre de mil ocho cientos ochenta y
> cuatro, hijo legítimo de **Don Luis Lerena Lenguas** y de **Doña Julia Juanicó, orientales**:
> abuelos paternos: **Don Luis** y **Doña Justiniana Lenguas**: maternos: **Don Cándido** y **Doña
> Juliana González**: fueron padrinos: **Don Jaime Herrera** y **Doña María Lerena**…*

Four things come out of it that no index carried:

1. **It closes an open question.** This archive had asked who Cándido Juanicó's wife was, because a
   published genealogy gives *Sixta Lenguas González*, married 1857 — too late for a daughter
   marrying in 1864. The act says **Juliana González**. That is now three acts in the same parish
   saying so, across twenty-two years. The registers disagree with the published genealogy, and the
   registers were written at the time.

2. **The godparents are the family.** The godmother, *Doña María Lerena*, is the baby's own eldest
   sister — María Julia Margarita, born at **Rosario** in 1865. The godfather, *Don Jaime Herrera*,
   is the man she married **in this same parish four months later**, on 3 November 1886. They stood
   together at the font while betrothed.

3. **Both parents are stated *orientales*** — Uruguayan — in their own parish, twenty-two years
   after the marriage act said the same thing.

4. **He was baptised at twenty-one months**, not as an infant. That habit matters well beyond this
   family: the whole search for Pablo Armando assumes a baptism near the birth. See
   [[the-index-before-1879]] and [[the-register-itself]].

## The index was made by a computer, and it was wrong

FamilySearch says so on the record. It rendered the parents as *"Julia Juanico"* and *"Luis Lorena"*
and marked **both as Mother**. It also filed the act under **LORENA**. Anyone searching LERENA and
trusting the index would never have seen this child. The image settles all of it.

That is the rule this archive already keeps, arriving from the other direction: an index is a
finding aid, and the act is the evidence. See [[lerena-evidence-discipline]].

## What it does not do

It does not find Juan Carlos. Seven children, and none of them is he. The Montevideo household is
now very well documented and he is still not in it — which is itself worth more than it looks,
because it was the strongest remaining place to put him.
