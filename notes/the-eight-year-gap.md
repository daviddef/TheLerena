# The eight-year gap — narrowed to two ships

## The gap
**A pupil teacher at Northam, Devon, in April 1901.** *(1901 census: "Mary Taylor, daughter, 16, School
teacher"; Exeter Diocesan Board examination results, Western Times, 4 April 1901.)*

**"Mrs M T Lerena", crossing from Cape Town to Southampton with a three-year-old son, in February 1909.**
*(TNA BT 26/398/29, SS Guelph, lines 17 and 18.)*

Between those two facts lie **eight years and six thousand miles**, and this archive has argued they are the
largest *answerable* hole in the family's story — larger than where Pablo Armando was born, and sitting in
records that survive and are indexed.

## What the outward lists say

***No Mary Taylor of her age left Britain for South Africa at all.*** A search of
**Passenger Lists Leaving UK 1890–1960** for *Mary Taylor*, born 1882–86, destination South Africa, across
the whole seventy-year range, returns **zero**.

**But the index frequently carries no forename.** Filtering on surname, age and destination alone — and
dropping the forename — produces **two candidates, both in exactly the right window**:

| | Age | Marital | Occupation | Sailed | Ship | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **MISS Taylor** | **19** | S | ***Domestic*** | Southampton → **Cape, 9 September 1903** | ***Harlech Castle*** (Donald Currie & Co, 254 passengers, master J. George) | TNA **BT 27/0420**, p.68, img 0004F |
| **MISS Taylor** | **19** | S | — | Southampton → **Cape, 9 April 1904** | ***Walmer Castle*** (Union-Castle, 271 passengers, master R. Rendall) | TNA **BT 27/0448** |

## Why the first one is worth a second look
**Mary Septima Taylor was born on 17 September 1884.** On **9 September 1903** she was **eight days short
of her nineteenth birthday** — and the manifest records the passenger as **19**.

And the occupation fits the life. She was a **pupil teacher** at sixteen, in a **cab driver's** household of
ten children at Northam. After the South African War the Cape was **actively recruiting British domestic
servants**, and passage was assisted. **A north Devon girl of nineteen going out to the Cape as a domestic
in 1903 is not a romantic story — it is the ordinary one**, and it would put her in Cape Town roughly two
years before Roque was born there in November 1905.

## *** But this is a candidate, not an identification ***
**Taylor is among the commonest surnames in England**, and "Miss Taylor, 19" will have sailed for the Cape
many times in those years. Two records fitting a window is not proof, and nothing here should be repeated
as though it were.

## How to settle it
**The original manifests.** The index carries no forename, but the **image very often does** — a forename
or at least initials. Both references are given above and both images are on findmypast.
