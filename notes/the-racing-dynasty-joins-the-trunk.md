# The racing dynasty joins the trunk

*13 September 2026. Index-level; the images are not yet opened.*

**Gilberto Justiniano Lerena Lenguas** — co-founder of the Argentine Stud Book, owner of Haras El
Viejo, the man the *Gran Premio Gilberto Lerena* at Palermo was renamed for in 1914 — has been in
this archive since the first week, as an *unplaced* Lerena of high interest. He was interesting for
an obvious reason: Pablo Armando trained and owned racehorses, and here was a Lerena at the centre
of Argentine thoroughbred breeding, in the right country at the right time.

He was never connected to anything. Today his parents were named.

## The finding

Searching Uruguayan parish records on **Justiniana Lenguas as mother** returns eight rows. Two of
them are records of Gilberto's own children — one with his wife given as *Julia Salvanach*, one as
*Julia Salvancich* — and **both give his parents as Luis and Justiniana Lenguas**.

That is the same couple who are the parents of **Luis Eugenio Lerena Lenguas**, stated on his death
act of 1901 and on his marriage act of 1864.

**So Gilberto is a brother of Luis Eugenio.** The household is:

- **Don Luis Lerena (y Fernández)** × **Doña Justiniana Lenguas** (b. c.1822, alive at 79 in 1901)
  - Luis Eugenio, b. 1843 — married Julia Juanicó, 1864; d. 24 Dec 1901
  - Justinia, b. 1843 (as indexed)
  - Alberto Otario, b. 1850
  - **Gilberto**, b. c.1854 — married Julia Salvanach
  - María Ema, b. 1857

And above them, from a separate record, **Don Luis Lerena is himself the son of Sancho and María
Fernández** — see [[sancho-of-llerena]].

## Why it matters

The one Lerena baptised at Rosario before 1879 — the single name in 148 years of that index — was a
daughter of Luis Eugenio, born there in 1865 to parents recorded as *naturales del E. Oriental*.
This archive has spent days establishing that Pablo Armando's people were **not a Rosario family**
but a Uruguayan one that passed through.

That same Uruguayan household, it now turns out, produced **the man who co-founded the Argentine
Stud Book and moved to Buenos Aires between 1887 and 1889**. The horses and the Rosario thread are
not two coincidences. They are one family.

## What this does NOT establish

- **It does not find Juan Carlos.** Five siblings are now named in that generation and none is he.
- **It does not make Pablo Armando a descendant of any of them.** Nothing yet connects him to this
  household. The enrolment card gives his father as Juan Carlos Lerena, and Juan Carlos is still in
  no indexed record in Argentina or Uruguay.
- **It is index-level.** Two independent parish index entries agree, which is better than one, but
  neither image has been opened. Until they are, this is **SECONDARY**, and it is recorded as a
  strong lead rather than a proved descent. See [[lerena-evidence-discipline]].
