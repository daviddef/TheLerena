# The Taylors had horses too

**Source:** 1891 and 1901 censuses of Northam, Devon, read on findmypast 9 September 2026.
1901 is RG13, piece 2163, folio 71, page 15, schedule 99 — **North Street, Northam**.

## The correction, and it is a good one

This archive has been carrying **William Taylor** — Mary Septima's father — as a **coachman**, taken from
David's MyHeritage tree. The censuses say something more specific and much more interesting.

**1891, aged 65, his occupation is entered as:**

> ***"Cab driver retired sergt p cavaloy"***

That is a census enumerator's compression of **cab driver; retired sergeant, [—?] Cavalry**. In **1901**,
aged 75 and a widower, he is simply an ***army pensioner***.

**So Mary Septima's father was a retired cavalry sergeant who drove a cab.** His eldest son William J was
a **cab driver** beside him, and his second son Horace was a **footman**. This was a household that made
its living from horses and from service.

## Why that is worth more than a footnote

The family story about Pablo Armando is entirely a horse story: he brought Argentine breeding to South
Africa, he was a *Trainer* on his own passport, a *Race Horse Owner* on his death certificate, he built
stables on the Koeberg Road, he took apprentices, and his descendants have been jockeys and trainers for
four generations.

**It now turns out that the woman he married came from horses as well.** Her father spent his working life
in the saddle and on the box — a cavalry sergeant, then a cab proprietor's driver in a small Devon town.
Two people from opposite ends of the earth, and the same trade standing behind both of them.

That is not evidence of anything. It does not explain how they met and it proves nothing about him. But
this archive has spent a long time treating Mary Septima as the ordinary English half of the story, and
that is now plainly wrong. **Horses are on both sides of this marriage.**

## Two other things the censuses give

**Her mother was a Londoner.** *Mary A H Taylor*, wife, 47 in 1891, is recorded as born in
**London, Middlesex** — not Devon. The tree gives her as **Mary Ann Hellen Brayley, b. 20 May 1843**, and
Brayley is a north Devon surname, so the family is Devon by name; but she herself was born in the capital.
Worth resolving.

**Mary Septima was a schoolteacher at sixteen, and the census says so independently.** The 1901 census was
taken on **31 March 1901**; the *Western Times* printed the Exeter Diocesan Board examination results on
**4 April 1901**. Four days apart, two unrelated sources, the same fact: *Mary Taylor, daughter, 16,
**School teacher**, born Northam.* The pupil-teacher finding is now corroborated.

## And one genuine anomaly, recorded rather than explained
***Mary Septima is missing from the 1891 census.*** She was born in September 1884, so she should be a
six-year-old at North Street on census night 1891, between her sister Lucy (9) and her sister Agnes (1).
She is not there. A search of the whole of Devon for a Mary Taylor born 1883–1885 returns four girls, none
of whom is her.

Three ordinary explanations exist — she was away from home that night, she was enumerated under a name the
index has mangled, or findmypast's transcription simply drops her. Any of them is more likely than anything
dramatic. **But it is an absence, it is recorded here, and it should be checked against the original image
before anyone builds on the household list above.**
