# The nationality chain — independent proof that Pablo Armando was Argentine

One of the oldest doubts in this project is whether Pablo Armando was really an Argentine national, or
whether the family simply believed it. The Argentine passport of 1940 says he was — but it was issued when
he was 58, in Cape Town, and we have never seen what he produced to obtain it.

**Two British passenger manifests, twenty-six years apart, settle it from the outside.**

| | Mary Septima's declared nationality |
|---|---|
| **19 February 1909**, SS *Guelph*, Cape Town → Southampton | **English** |
| **20 May 1935**, RMS *Balmoral Castle*, Cape Town → Southampton | **"Agentine"** *(Argentine, as spelt by the purser)* |

Mary Septima Taylor was born at **Northam, Devon**, and baptised there on **30 October 1884**. She was
English by birth and nothing changed that fact. What changed was **her husband's nationality becoming
hers**: under the British Nationality Act 1870 and the practice of the period, a British woman who married
a foreign national **took his nationality and lost her own**. She and Pablo Armando married in
**September 1911** — between these two voyages.

So: **English before the marriage, Argentine after it.** The only thing that can have made an English
Devon woman Argentine is the nationality of the man she married. A ship's purser in 1935, with no interest
in this family and no knowledge of its stories, wrote down that she was Argentine.

**That is independent, contemporaneous, official corroboration that Pablo Armando Lerena was an Argentine
national** — arrived at without relying on his own passport, on family memory, or on anything he said
about himself.

## It does not settle where he was *born*
Nationality and birthplace are different things. He could have been born in Uruguay or Spain and
naturalised Argentine, which would sit with the Rosario baptism index refusing to hold him, with the
Uruguayan density of the surname, and with the *Ley de Residencia* reading of the family's
political-refugee story. **What is now beyond reasonable doubt is that he held Argentine nationality by
1911** — not merely by 1940.

## A second thing the 1909 record proves
The manifest of 19 February 1909 lists, on **consecutive lines**:

- **line 17 — Mrs M T Lerena**, female, English
- **line 18 — Mast. L Lerena**, male, British Colonial

"Mast." is *Master* — a boy. **Roque Luis Armando Lerena was born at Cape Town on 25 November 1905**, so he
was three years old in February 1909, and his father's 1950 estate papers call him *"LOUIS Lerena, major
son"*. The initial fits, the age fits, the family fits.

**So Mary Septima was already being recorded as "Mrs Lerena" in February 1909 — two and a half years
before the 1911 marriage** — and she was travelling to England with their small son.

That is exactly the pattern this family has already shown once: **Roque was born in November 1905, his
parents did not marry until September 1911**, and the marriage register calls them bachelor and spinster.
The 1911 marriage was the regularisation of a household that had existed for years, not its beginning.

It also complicates the family story that the couple *met* on a ship to South Africa. By 1909 she was
crossing between the Cape and England as Mrs Lerena with a child. Whatever happened, it happened before
this.
