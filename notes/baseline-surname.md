# Lerena — baseline surname research

Established 2026-09-08, before the MyHeritage tree was read. Nothing here is about David's line
specifically; it is the backdrop against which that line has to be placed.

## The headline fact: this name is RARE

Forebears (accessed 2026-09-08): **2,124 bearers worldwide**, 197,177th commonest surname on earth,
incidence **1:3,431,048**.

Compare the sibling archives: **Falco has 46,749** bearers and two independent origins, so no two Falco
lines may be assumed connected. **Lerena is 22x scarcer.** The methodological consequence inverts:

> With a surname this rare, a cluster of Lerenas in one place is far more likely to be one family than
> coincidence. Distribution may **propose** connections. Only registers may **prove** them.

### Read by density, not total
- Spain has the most in absolute terms — expected of a country of origin.
- **Uruguay's density is the world's highest**, ~6.6x Spain's. That is the classic **founder-emigration**
  signature: few arrived, descendants now a measurable fraction of a small population.
- **River Plate (Uruguay + Argentina) is the modern heartland.** The family's Rosario claim lands inside it.
- **South Africa's 61 = ~3% of all Lerenas alive**, in a country with no Spanish colonial past and no
  River Plate migration stream. Almost certainly **one family that arrived and multiplied**.

## The two-origins fork — UNRESOLVED

1. **Basque house-name.** `-ena` is the Basque possessive, "house of" (cf. Perurena, Marinelarena,
   Arruabarrena). Fits Basque emigration to the River Plate from the 1820s; up to 10% of Uruguayans carry
   a Basque surname.
2. **Castilian toponymic.** **Llerena**, Badajoz, Extremadura — seat of the Order of Santiago and of an
   Inquisitorial tribunal, large medieval Jewish/converso population. **36,828 bearers of Llerena**;
   Forebears lists it as the closest variant at 92% similarity. `Ll-` → `L-` reduces readily in
   Spanish-American registers and at ports.

**This is the Falco/De Falco problem again**, and it is closed the same way: in the registers.

Complication: Forebears summarises the Spanish Lerenas as chiefly **La Rioja, Andalusia and Madrid** —
neither Basque nor Extremaduran, though La Rioja borders the Basque country and Andalusia is where
Extremadurans went. **Needs the INE provincial figures, not a summary.**

## How to close it
- Earliest River Plate records: which spelling, and do the two forms alternate for one person?
- INE provincial distribution for Spain.
- The emigration record — normally names the town of origin outright, settling it in one line.

## Caution on secondary sites

Such sites routinely invent etymologies. Not relied on.
