# The L section, closed

*10 September 2026. David asked for this to be done properly so it could be left behind. It has been.*

## What was actually outstanding

The Rosario cathedral baptism index had been swept **letter by letter for 1881, 1882 and 1883** — every
letter, looking for the forename *Pablo Armando* under any surname. That is a different question from the
one the enrolment card now poses. The card gives a **father called Lerena**, so the question becomes:
**does the surname appear in Rosario at all, in any year?**

Against that question the record had gaps. The L band was noted as read for 1882 and, in one line without
detail, for "1883–1895". Nothing was recorded for **1879, 1880, or 1896–1900**, and the Ll section had
been read only for **1880–1884**.

## What was done

**Every opening of the L section and of the Ll section, from the first to the last, in order** — 41
frames, of which 38 are distinct and 3 are duplicate exposures. Each was verified on arrival by its year
and its Libro/folio span, and the spans run continuously from **Libro 23, folio 145** to **Libro 48,
folio 546**. Recorded frame by frame in `data/rosario-index-L-complete.tsv`.

## The result

> ### No LERENA. No LLERENA. No LARENA. No LORENA.
> ### Not in any year from 1879 to 1901. Not in either section.

The L section opens at **1880** — there are **no L entries at all for 1879** — and runs past 1900 into
1901. The whole Ll section, across twenty-two years, amounts to **twenty-six entries**: Llanos, Llorca,
Llanes, Llaurer, Llanar, Llona, Llana, Llosa, Llopis, Llovera, Llorenti, Llobet, Lluch, Llull, Lluró,
Llovet, Llorente, Llopas, Llulli, Llure, and two **Lleras**. Not one Llerena.

Names that come close and are not it, recorded so they are not found again and mistaken for something:
*Lorenzo, Lorenzini, Lorenzetti, Lorensa, Loresano, Loresangui, Lorense, Ladinas* — and, nearest of all,
**LLERA**: *Pedro*, 1898, folio 318, and *Ida C.*, 1900, folio 191.

## The Armandos

The forename this archive has spent a week arguing is rare does exist at Rosario — four times in the L
band alone, and never in the right year:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lofré, Armando** | 1889, folio 230 |
| **Luján, Armando** | 1895, folio 53 |
| **Lunardi, Armando** | 1895, folio 65 |
| **Limonetti, Armando** | 1897, folio 504 |

All late, all after 1889. That is worth knowing on its own: *Armando* looks like a name that came into
fashion at Rosario in the 1890s, which makes a boy christened Pablo **Armando** in February 1882 slightly
unusual for his date — and slightly more likely to have been named for someone.

## The method, which is the transferable part

The index is **typewritten, not handwritten**. The obstacle was never the hand; it was the viewer, whose
zoom would not go far enough to read a page.

FamilySearch serves each page as **DeepZoom tiles**:

```
https://sg30p0.familysearch.org/service/records/storage/deepzoomcloud/dz/v1/{ark}/image_files/{level}/{col}_{row}.jpg
```

Level 13 is the maximum for this film — **20 × 15 tiles of 257 px**, about **5100 × 3800** for a two-page
opening. Assembling those tiles into a plain HTML grid at 42% scale puts a whole opening on one screen at
a size where every surname is legible, and at 100% a single column is as clear as print.

**Image numbers are not to be trusted** — they drift between viewer states, and the number box and the
underlying ark fall out of step. The **image ark** is stable, and that is what the data file records.

## What this settles, and what it does not

**It settles that the surname is not in the Rosario cathedral index.** Not thinly present, not
misspelled into something adjacent — absent, across twenty-two years, in a section read end to end. Until
1888 the cathedral was the only parish in Rosario. A boy born there in February 1882 to a father named
Lerena should be in that book.

**It does not settle where he was baptised.** Three possibilities remain, and the archive should stop
treating this as a null and start treating it as a question:

1. **He was not baptised as an infant.** He married in a Catholic church in 1911 after banns, so he was
   baptised at some point — but not necessarily at three days old, and not necessarily in Rosario.
2. **He was baptised outside the cathedral's books** — a hospital, a private chapel, a military chaplain,
   or a parish outside the city that served the family's actual address.
3. **The index itself is incomplete.** It is a later typescript compiled from the registers, not the
   registers themselves. Everything in this sweep depends on whoever typed it having missed nothing.

The third is the one nobody likes to say aloud, and it is the reason the **act-by-act** reading of the
1882 register — as opposed to its index — is still worth doing one day.

**The gap is now precisely bounded, and that is the point.** It can be left behind.
