# Córdoba — and an Armando born in 1882

## Why Córdoba at all
**Bartolomé Llerena**, the only person of this surname found living in Rosario before 1882, was **born in
Córdoba in 1824**. So Córdoba is where he came from — and it had never been searched.

## There is a Llerena family at Córdoba Cathedral
Documented across the nineteenth century in *Argentina, Córdoba, Catholic Church Records 1557–1974*:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Francisco LLERENA** m. **Rafaela Daraz** | → **Baldomero Llerena**, b. **1851** |
| **Baldomero Llerena** m. **Margarita Correas**, 16 February 1876, Catedral Nuestra Señora de la Asunción | → **Sahara** (d. 1897), **Carlos** (d. 1878), **Julia Rosa** |
| **Baldomero** with **Leonarda Pereyra** | → **Amalia Llerena** |
| **Rafael LLERENA** m. **Rosario Moyano** | → Rudecinda |
| **Lucrecia LLERENA** m. Vicente Campellone | → Vicente Serafín |

**Bartolomé, born at Córdoba in 1824, belongs to the generation of Francisco and Rafael** — plausibly a
brother. This is a real, previously unexamined family in the province our one Rosario man came from.

***But there is no Pablo and no Armando among them.***

## *** And then this ***
Searching *Armando* against the surname turned up, in the **Argentine military records 1911–1936**:

> ***Armand[o] LORENA*** — **born 1882** — birthplace ***Rosario*** de Lerma, Salta —
> father **Juan Carlos Lorena Serena**, mother ***María LERENA***

Set out plainly, the coincidences are hard to ignore:

- **Born 1882** — our man's year.
- **Armando** — our man's second name, and a name this project has shown to be *rare*: two in three whole
  years of the Rosario baptism index, and **none at all** in four centuries of Spanish records.
- Born at a place called ***Rosario***.
- **His mother's surname is LERENA** — and the **mother's-surname hypothesis** has been live in this
  archive for days, on the evidence that this family had children before marrying.
- He is in the **military records** — exactly where a man of the *clase 1882* should be, and the passport
  records **Guardia Nacional, 6º de Caballería, 1915**.

## The objections, which are serious
1. ***Rosario de Lerma is in SALTA*** — some 1,400 km north-west of Rosario de Santa Fe. **The passport
   names the province**: *"Rosario de Santa Fe"*. These are different towns and the record is explicit.
2. The name is **Armando**, not **Pablo Armando**.
3. The surname is **Lorena**, the father's — not Lerena.
4. **FamilySearch states the record was indexed by a computer.** "Lorena Serena" reads like machine
   mangling, and machine indexes of Spanish military hands are unreliable.

## Verdict
***Probably not him — and worth checking anyway.*** The birthplace mismatch is the sort of thing that
usually kills a candidate outright, and it should be allowed to. But **Armando + 1882 + a Rosario + a
mother née Lerena, in the military records**, is a conjunction this project has not seen before, and the
transcription is admitted to be machine-made.

**The original document would settle it in a minute** — it is linked from the record and has not been
opened. Recorded as a candidate, not a finding.
