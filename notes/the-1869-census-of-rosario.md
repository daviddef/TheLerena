# The 1869 census of Rosario — a household that may be ours

**Found 9 September 2026**, while working the Santa Rosa de Lima parish. It is the first time this archive
has found **anyone of this surname living in Rosario before 1882**.

## What the 1869 National Census shows at Rosario

| Name as indexed | Born | Birthplace |
|---|---|---|
| ***Bartolomé LLERENA*** | **1824** | **Córdoba** |
| Agustín **Lorena** | 1829 | — |
| Guillermo **Lorena** | 1853 | Córdoba |
| Gerónima **Lorena** | 1854 | Córdoba |
| Mercedes **Lorena** | 1860 | Córdoba |
| Feliz **Lorena** | 1866 | Córdoba |
| María **Lorena** | 1868 | Córdoba |
| Luis **Loroña** | 1846 | — |
| José **Loroña** | 1847 | — |
| (infant) **Loroña** | 1868 | Santa Fe |

Read that as a group and a shape appears: **Agustín (b.1829) with children born at Córdoba between 1853 and
1868** — a family that moved from **Córdoba to Rosario** during the 1860s — and **Bartolomé (b.1824),
also Córdoba-born**, of the same generation as Agustín.

## Why this matters
Pablo Armando's passport says he was born at **Rosario on 22 February 1882**. Everything this archive has
done since has failed to find a Lerena *in Rosario at all*: not in the cathedral baptisms for 1881–83 under
any surname, not in the marriage registers 1868–91, not in the 1895 census, not in Argentine baptisms
generally.

**Here, for the first time, is the surname living in the city — thirteen years before he was born.**
Bartolomé, born 1824, would be **58 in 1882**. Agustín, born 1829, would be **53**. Either is old enough to
be his **grandfather**; the children born in the 1850s are exactly the right age to be his **parents**.

## *** The obvious objection, and it is a real one ***
**"Lorena" is a real and separate Spanish surname.** So is *Loroña*. The index may simply be correct, and
these may be three unrelated families with nothing to do with us.

But three things make the transcription worth doubting:
1. The same small group is indexed under **three different spellings** — *Llerena*, *Lorena*, *Loroña* —
   which is the signature of an indexer struggling with one hand.
2. **Lerena, Lorena and Llerena differ by a single letter**, and this project has already watched
   *Belmonte* be read as *Sarmiento* and *Laurini* be read as *Larena*.
3. The 1869 census is among the roughest-transcribed sets on FamilySearch.

**This is a hypothesis, not a finding, and it is recorded as one.**

## How to settle it — and it is settleable
**Look at the census image.** The 1869 schedules are digitised. The handwriting will show whether the name
is *Lerena*, *Lorena* or *Llerena*, and the schedule will show **the household structure** — who is head,
who is wife, who are children, and their ages — which the index strips out.

That is one image, and it would either hand us a Lerena household in Rosario in 1869 or kill the idea
outright. **It is now the most valuable single unexamined document in the Argentine work.**

## Two other threads from the same search
- ***Gabino L. Llerena*** — a groom in the **Uruguay Catholic Church Records**, whose **birthplace is given
  as ROSARIO**. A Rosario-born Llerena who married in Uruguay ties the two countries together in exactly
  the way this family's evidence keeps hinting at.
- ***Camilo Llerena***, 1869 census at **Nogoyá, Entre Ríos**, **born 1842 in Santa Fe** — a Santa Fe-born
  Llerena living in Entre Ríos, which is where the large **Gualeguaychú Larena kindred** sits.

## *** The correction ***
**The page above proposed a "household" — Agustín with children born at Córdoba — and that reading was
wrong.** There is no such household here. **Every line carries its own number**, and the twelve people are
of a dozen different surnames: washerwomen, a laundress, day labourers, a peon, a carpenter, a Spanish
shopkeeper. This is a **list of individuals at one address — a conventillo, or lodgings** — not a family.

The *Lorena* and *Loroña* entries I grouped with him are **elsewhere in the census**, not on this page.
Line 10 here, which the index led me to expect as a Lorena, is **Vachalo**.

***So there is no Lerena family in Rosario in 1869.*** There is **one man**.

## What does survive, and it is still worth having
> ***Bartolomé LLERENA — 45 years old, married, Argentine, born in CÓRDOBA, working as a JORNALERO —
> living in Rosario, Sección 2ª, at the national census of 1869.***

He remains **the only person of this surname found in Rosario before 1882**, in any record this project has
searched. He would be **58 in 1882**.

**But three things temper it:**
1. **He is lodging among strangers.** No wife appears beside him though he is recorded married, and no
   children. Whatever family he had is not on this page.
2. **He is a day labourer.** Not a horseman, not a breeder, not a man of property. If Pablo Armando
   descends from him, the family's rise in one generation is far steeper than the family story implies.
3. **The surname reads *Llerena*** on the page — with the Castilian double L, the Extremaduran toponymic
   form — not *Lerena*.

## Where that leaves it
The lead is **weaker than it looked last night, and still the best pre-1882 Rosario evidence there is.**
The next step is not this page but the rest of the census: **find whether Bartolomé appears again in 1869
with a household**, and whether the *Lorena/Loroña* entries are a family or, as here, scattered lodgers.
That is a search of the same 344-image set, and it is now a much more modest hope than it was.
