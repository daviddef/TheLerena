# The only grave

*13 September 2026. FamilySearch had signed us out, so the afternoon went to the untried non-FamilySearch
sources. The third one produced the first physical trace of this family anywhere.*

## What it is

A **grave kerb** at **Maitland, Woltemade Cemetery, Cape Town** — poured concrete edging, not a
headstone — with one word cut into its face in inset lettering:

> ## LERENA

**And nothing else.** No forename, no dates, no epitaph. Above the kerb, bare earth and grass. Whatever
marked the plot is gone, or was never there.

Photographed by **Heather MacAlister** and published in the **eGGSA Gravestone Photograph Library** —
album *"Le — surnames starting with the letters Le"*, image 85 of 127, page dated 5 May 2021. Eighty-seven
people had looked at it before this archive did.

## Why it matters

**Maitland Woltemade is where both of them are buried.**

- **Mary Septima**, November 1935, **grave 7719A**, Roman Catholic, Cemetery no. 1 — from the Maitland
  Road interment register, p.3215 entry 163.
- **Pablo Armando**, 19 March 1950 — *"Intended place of burial: Maitland Cemetery no 1"*, from the death
  certificate.

And this is the **only Lerena grave in the whole Western Cape collection**. The variants were checked the
same afternoon: **LERIN** — which would catch Lerina and Lerini — returns **nothing**; *Lerena* as a
substring also covers *Llerena*, and there is no separate hit; **LAREN** returns 39 records, all Clarence
and its like.

## What it does not say

**It cannot be assigned to a person.** A surname on a kerb, with no dates and no forename, is consistent
with Mary Septima's grave, with Pablo Armando's, or with a family plot holding both — and this archive
does not turn "consistent with" into "is".

**What would settle it** is the grave number. Mary Septima's is recorded as **7719A**. The Cape Town
municipal cemetery office holds the Woltemade register; a section and row against that kerb would tie it
to one of them in a minute.

## And one small thing it does say

**The stone says LERENA.**

That bears, lightly, on the hypothesis raised from the DNA: that the family's name might be nearer
**LERINA** — the spelling on his death certificate, and an Italian surname at CEMLA. Whoever paid for
this kerb, and whoever cut it, spelled it **Lerena**.

It is a small point and it is not decisive — the kerb may postdate him, and a mason follows the order he
is given. But it is evidence, and it runs against a hypothesis this archive put up eleven days ago.
