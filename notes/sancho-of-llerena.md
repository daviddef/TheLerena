# Sancho *de* Llerena

*13 September 2026, late. Hypothesis 1 named the one thing that would settle it. Tonight that thing
turned up — and it went the way the archive had judged less likely.*

## What hypothesis 1 asked for

Since 8 September this archive has carried a proposition about the surname itself:

> **That *Lerena* is a Basque-formed house-name of the upper Ebro, not a reduction of *Llerena* in
> Badajoz.**
>
> *To settle it:* the La Rioja parish registers; **and whether *Lerena* and *Llerena* ever alternate for
> one person in early River Plate records.**

The case *for* was real and remains so. Spain has exactly one dense cluster of **Lerena** and it is
**La Rioja**, at **39.6× the national density** — the upper Ebro, immediately south of the Basque
provinces, where Basque house-names in **-ena** ("the house of") spill into Castilian country.
Extremadura does not appear in the Spanish distribution at all.

## What turned up

Following the 1866 Rosario act back through the Montevideo family produced a trunk, and at the root of
it a man with fourteen Uruguayan parish records attached to him:

> **Sancho Llerena Aguilar**, born **Córdoba, Andalusia, Spain, 1767**; a soldier; married
> **María Josefa Fernández Tresguerras** at **Canelones, Uruguay, 8 January 1791**; resident at
> Montevideo; died 1832.

He is the great-grandfather of the Lerena Lenguas and the Lerena Traibel — and therefore of
**Luis Lerena Lenguas**, the father in the Rosario act, and of **Gilberto Justiniano Lerena Lenguas**
of the Argentine Stud Book.

And here is how the parish registers of one country spell his name, across forty years:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1793 | Sancho **Lerena** |
| 1793 | Sancho **Lerena** |
| 1804 | Sancio **Llerena** |
| 1811 | Sancho **Llerena** |
| 1827 | Sancho **Llerena** |
| **1828** | **Sancho *de* Llerena** |
| 1830 | Sancho **Llerena** |
| 1840 | D. Sancho **Lerena** |
| 1858 | Sancho **Lerenes** |
| 1858 | Sancho **Lerena** |

**Lerena and Llerena, alternating, for one man, in one country's registers.** That is exactly the test
hypothesis 1 set for itself.

And the 1828 form is the strongest single item in the list: ***Sancho de Llerena*** — the toponymic
construction, *of Llerena*, which is how the Badajoz town-name behaves when it is used as a surname. A
Basque house-name in *-ena* does not take *de*.

## What this does and does not settle

**It does not overturn the Spanish distribution.** La Rioja really is where the spelling *Lerena*
clusters in Spain today, and that cluster has to be something. The honest synthesis is that **both
readings can be true at once**: a genuinely Basque-formed *Lerena* of the upper Ebro, and — separately —
a River Plate *Lerena* that is a **reduction of Andalusian *Llerena***, carried across by a soldier from
Córdoba in the 1780s and smoothed to one *l* by Uruguayan clerks within a generation.

**For this archive, only the second one matters.** The question was never where the name is dense in
Spain; it was where *this family's* name came from. And the only Lerena trunk yet found in the River
Plate is a Llerena.

**It does not connect to Pablo Armando.** No Juan Carlos appears anywhere in this tree. The trunk
belongs to the Montevideo family, and nothing yet places his father in it.

**And it is secondary.** The FamilySearch Tree is user-submitted and editable by anyone; the
*attachments* — one user's decision that these fourteen records are one man — have not been checked
against the acts themselves. What is solid, and checkable by anyone who looks, is the narrower fact:
**the Uruguayan parish registers spell this family both ways.**

## The practical consequence

Every sweep this archive has run has treated **Llerena** as a variant worth searching. It now has a
documented reason to treat it as **the earlier form** — which changes what an empty *L-e-r* result
means. The Rosario indexes were read for Lerena, Llerena, Larena and Lorena together, so nothing needs
redoing there. But in Montevideo, the register to open first is the one under **Ll**.

## A measurement, added the same day

The two spellings can be counted. FamilySearch's **full-text** corpus reads handwriting across
un-indexed images and covers both Montevideo and Buenos Aires; its Place filter gives per-country
counts. Searching each surname as an exact phrase:

| | Argentina | Uruguay | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LERENA** | 701 | **4,517** | **Uruguay 6.4× Argentina** |
| **LLERENA** | **3,415** | 947 | Argentina 3.6× Uruguay |
| *Fernández* (reference) | 608,205 | 305,473 | Argentina 2.0× Uruguay |
| *Bustamante* (reference) | 75,647 | 9,328 | Argentina 8.1× Uruguay |

A control is necessary because the two national corpora are not the same size and every surname has
its own geography — so the comparison is made against **two reference surnames measured the same way**
rather than against an assumed corpus ratio.

**Both references run Argentina-heavy** — one by twice, one by eight times. **So does *Llerena*.**
***Lerena* is the only one of the four that reverses**, and it reverses hard.

It is also **the spelling this family used**.

And it sits neatly with Sancho: a **Llerena** of Córdoba marries at Canelones in 1791, his descendants
are written **both ways** in the Uruguayan registers for forty years, and today the single-*l* form is
the Uruguayan one while the double-*l* form stayed Argentine.

**What it does not say**, plainly: this measures a digitised corpus, not a population. Coverage is
uneven between countries and periods and the handwriting recognition misreads. It is evidence about
where the *name* lives, not about where any particular family lived, and it cannot place Juan Carlos
Lerena anywhere at all.
