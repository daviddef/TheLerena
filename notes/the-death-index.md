# The death index

*13 September 2026. The biggest untried record set in Rosario, opened at last — and it answers two
thirds of the question and then runs out of pages.*

## Why it was the right thing to open

Every Rosario sweep this archive has run has been for **baptisms** and **marriages**. Nobody had opened
the deaths. And a death act does the same work as a baptism act: **it names the parents of the
deceased.** If **Juan Carlos Lerena** or **María Lerena** died at Rosario, the death index is where they
would be found, and it would name *their* parents — pushing the line back a generation in a single
entry.

## What it is

**"Índice de defunciones 1800–1888", Parroquia Nuestra Señora del Rosario. Eighty-six images.**
Handwritten — not the 1945 typescript that indexes the baptisms — four columns to a page, *surname ·
given name · folio*, grouped by letter and then by *libro* and year.

And it is **not one index**. It is a composite, bound together:

| Images | What it is |
|---|---|
| 3–44 | **Death index, first sequence** — A to Z, libros 4–7, **1800–1839** |
| 45–53 | **Death index, second sequence** — "Letra A" to "Letra S", **1882–1888** |
| ~51–55 | A further index whose entries read *"X **con** Y"* — **marriages**, not deaths |
| 56–58 | **"Bautismos de los Yndios"** — indigenous baptisms, letters A–P |
| 59–86 | **Death index, third sequence** — libros 4–8, c.1838–1869 — **and it stops at F** |

## What was read, and what was found

**The L section of the first sequence, images 27–29, 1800–1839.** Read in full. The names are
Lencinas, Leguizamón, López, Ludueña, Lucena, León, Luján, Llanos, Lescano, Lavallén, Lozano, Lemos,
Luna, Lira, Luque, Loza, Lazo, Latorre, Lagos, Livia, Lumbre, Larrea, Langüines, Lisarda — hundreds of
them. One near-miss logged: **Loverra, María (1820)** — *Lov-*, not *Ler-*.

> **No Lerena.**

**The L section of the second sequence, image 48, 1882–1888.** Read in full — seventy-odd entries
across seven years: Lugones, Ludueña, Lobos, Luria, Ledesma, Lucero, Leguizamón, Loza, Lescano, Lunas,
Lemos, Limbo, Lejarza, Lazarte, Leahy, López, Llanes, León, Lazo, Lezcano, Lencina, Lavallén, Labao,
Losarto, Lubes.

> **No Lerena.** And this is the span that includes 1882 itself.

## And then the book runs out

The third sequence — the one that would cover **c.1840 to 1881** — runs A (images 59–67), B (68–71), C,
D (82–83), E (84), **F (85–86)**, and the volume ends.

> **There is no L section for 1840–1881 in this film.**

That is not a gap in the searching. **It is a gap in the record.** And it falls precisely where it hurts
most: a man and a woman who had a son at Rosario in February 1882 are most likely to have died in the
decades either side of it, and for the four decades before it the finding aid simply does not exist.

## What is left, and what it would cost

The death **registers** themselves do survive and are filmed — *Defunciones* runs in volumes to 1875.
So a Rosario death between 1841 and 1875 is still findable: it would have to be read **act by act**,
the way the 1882 baptism register was read. That is a far larger job than an index would have been, and
it is now the only route to it.

For deaths after 1875, the collection holds no death register at all.

## One method note

This index is handwritten and dense, and **level 13 drops tiles on this film** — eleven of one hundred
and eighty-seven on a single page-half, in regular horizontal bands, after ten retry passes. **Level 12
at 0.58 scale, one page-half per screenshot**, proved both complete and legible, and is the setting to
use here. Where level 13 did render, it was used to confirm individual names.
