# WikiTree

*13 September 2026. Asked for by David; checked, because the honest answer was no.*

## The gap was real

Before today the string "wikitree" appeared **nowhere** in this archive — not in `data/`, not
in `notes/`, not in the site. That is an odd omission, because the archive already leans on
Geneanet (14 mentions), FamilySearch Tree (20), MyHeritage (19), findmypast (14), Geni and
Ancestry. WikiTree had simply never been tried.

## WikiTree holds no Lerena

Zero profiles. The search returns *"Sorry, no WikiTree profile matches"*. Run a second time with
variant-matching switched off, so the null could not be the site quietly widening the spelling.

**Why that null is worth more than the usual null.** WikiTree is *one shared tree* rather than
millions of private ones. On Geni or MyHeritage, a name absent from your view means very little —
a hundred other users may hold it behind a paywall or a privacy setting. On WikiTree there is a
single public namespace. The surname is not in it. Zero is a measurement here in a way it is not
elsewhere.

LLERENA gives 26 non-living profiles, all Jalisco, Cuban-Floridian or Peruvian, plus one
**Antonio Llerena, b. c.1790 San Fernando, Cádiz, Andalucía** — Sancho's region and generation,
with no stated link and no River Plate descent. Not one Uruguayan or Argentine profile of either
spelling.

## So the English side was tested instead

This was the more honest test anyway. WikiTree is strongest on English parish families, and that
is exactly where this archive is weakest in *independence*: almost every fact on the Taylor line
traces back to a **single MyHeritage tree**. A second, independently built tree holding the same
people would be real corroboration.

- **Mary Septima Taylor** — not present. *Septima* as a given name returns 101 seventh daughters
  from South Carolina to Fremantle; none is hers.
- **Taylors of Worlingworth, Suffolk** — the nearest thing to a hit. Maria Anne (Taylor) Pringle
  b.1840 and Caroline (Taylor) Thistleton b.1841, both of Worlingworth, both emigrants to New
  South Wales. William Taylor was born in the same parish in Feb 1826, so they are plausibly his
  younger sisters. **All three profiles opened; every one has father unknown and mother unknown.**
  Nothing to attach them to in either direction.
- **Brayleys of Devon** — 100 profiles, a genuine north-Devon cluster (Chittlehampton, Swimbridge,
  Braunton, Filleigh, Okehampton) with emigration to Niagara and Buffalo. The surname's homeland,
  exactly where it should be, and no path into it from Mary Ann Hellen Brayley.
- **Mary Ann (Brayley) Yelland** b. c.1843 — the one true Brayley of the right year. Profile opened:
  Queensland, married a William Yelland before 1863, one son who died at Brisbane in 1945, parents
  unknown, and the whole profile resting on a single Queensland death-index entry naming his mother.
  **Not ours** — ours married William Taylor and died at Northam, Devon, on 21 April 1900.

## The tier

**SECONDARY**, with Geneanet, Geni and the FamilySearch Tree. User-built trees are leads, never
evidence — see [[lerena-evidence-discipline]].

WikiTree is *better made* than most of its peers: one namespace instead of duplicates, sources
demanded on the profile, and — as the Yelland profile showed — when a profile rests on one thin
source, you can see that it does. That is a virtue. It just does not help here.

## What would change this

Nothing about the Lerena side; the surname is absent and that is not going to shift. The one live
possibility is the **English side**: if someone ever attaches parents to the Worlingworth Taylors,
those two women become testable sisters of William Taylor, and the Taylor line stops resting on a
single tree. Worth re-running once, later. Not worth watching.
