# Cayastá — and the correction it forces

## What I said, and why it was wrong

Last night this archive recorded, with some excitement, that **Cayastá in Garay department, Santa Fe** held
"the only Larena/Lerena cluster anywhere in Santa Fe province" — an *Antonio Larena* with children baptised
in 1890 and 1892, in the same village as *Leon José Lerena*, baptised 1892.

**Looked at properly, the cluster dissolves.** It is almost certainly an artefact of transcription.

**Cayastá was an Italian agricultural colony.** Read the surrounding entries in its baptism register and
the point is unmistakable:

> Laurini · Laurini · Laurini · Laurini · Laurini · Lauron · Castelarin · Masorini · Panluzzi · Gaetta ·
> Salvaserra · Llorin · Mazi / Massi / Mossi

*Larena* sits in the middle of that. And the indexer was plainly struggling: **the same mother is
transcribed "Inés Azela" in one child's entry and "Inés Raiela" in the other.** A name rendered two
different ways in two consecutive acts is a name the transcriber could not read.

**"Antonio Larena of Cayastá" is most likely an Italian surname mis-rendered** — Laurini, Laurena or
similar — in a village full of exactly those names. The apparent coincidence of a Larena and a Lerena in
one small place is **the index mangling two different Italian families**, not a kindred.

*Recorded here rather than quietly deleted, because a wrong lead that has been chased and killed is worth
as much to the next person as a right one — and because this archive's whole method is to say what it
actually found.*

## What the same search turned up instead, and this part is real

Searching *Larena* across Argentina surfaced a **substantial, four-generation family at GUALEGUAYCHÚ, in
Entre Ríos**, densely documented in the Entre Ríos civil registration:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Juan Larena** m. **Petrona López** | → *Juan Antonio Larena*, b. 1837, d. 1 June 1908 |
| **Juan Antonio Larena** m. **Macedonia Lozano** | → *Victorino Severo Larena*, b. 1878, m. Plácida Epifania Alarcón, 4 May 1903 |
| **Victorino Larena** m. **Plácida Alarcón** | → Juan Antonio (1913), Josefa Epifania (21 Mar 1915) |
| **Vicente Antonio Larena** m. **Vicenta Mercedes Fuentes** | → Félix Antonio (1906), Aurora Eduarda (1913) |
| ***Roque Larena*** m. **Florencia Calixta Ramos** | → a child d. 1941 |
| **Tito Gregorio Larena** m. María Antonia Paredes | → Ramón Roberto (20 Oct 1932) |
| **Juan José Larena** m. María Justina Zapata | → Plácida Haydee (21 Jul 1937) |

At the **1895 national census** the family shows **Antonio Larena, b. 1876** and ***Roque Larena, b. 1881***
at Gualeguaychú. And it is at **Gualeguaychú** that the *other* of Argentina's two indexed Lerena baptisms
sits — ***Juan Pablo Lerena, 1891***.

### Why it caught my eye
***Roque.*** It is the name Pablo Armando gave his eldest son — **Roque Luis Armando Lerena**, born Cape
Town, November 1905 — and here is a **Roque Larena born in 1881**, within a year of Pablo Armando's stated
birth, in a family that also produces a **Juan Pablo**.

### And why it is not the answer
**There is no Pablo Larena and no Armando Larena anywhere in Entre Ríos.** Both searches return nothing but
fuzzy noise — *Loran, Lorin, Laurin, Larenz*. The Gualeguaychú family runs to Juan, Juan Antonio, Victorino,
Vicente Antonio, Roque, Tito Gregorio, Juan José, Félix Antonio, Aureliano Emiliano. **Neither of our two
names appears in four generations of it.**

And **Gualeguaychú is in Entre Ríos**, on the far side of the country from Rosario — which is what the
passport says, and the one thing about his origin the family has always been consistent about.

**So: recorded, not claimed.** *Roque* is a saint's name and a common enough one; a family that has no
Pablo and no Armando is not our family on the strength of it. This is the largest documented Larena
kindred in Argentina and it deserves to be on the register — but it is a candidate for elimination, not a
discovery, and it goes down as **unplaced**.
