# An Argentine diplomat named Lerena

**Found 9 September 2026** in the British inward passenger lists, under a findmypast trial.

On **17 November 1958** the *Libertad* docked in London from **La Plata, Argentina**. Disembarking in
first class, at item 49, line 5:

> **Carlos Gustavo LERENA**, born **20 January 1915**, married, **nationality Argentine**,
> **occupation: DIPLOMATIC**, last country of residence Argentina,
> ***future country of residence: IRAN***, address in Britain: Hotel Cumberland.

With him: **Nelly Maria Delvalle Lerena** (b. 1922), **Carlos Gilberto Lerena** (b. 1946) and
**Eleonora Lerena** (b. 1947) — evidently his wife and two children, in transit to a posting.

## Why this is worth a note

The oldest and least supported claim in this whole family history is that **Pablo Armando was Consul
General between Argentina and South Africa**. The archive has tested it and found nothing: no listing in
the Argentine diplomatic lists we have been able to reach, nothing in the South African official records,
and the suspicion — set out on the hypotheses page — that the tradition may have grown out of the 1940
**passport** itself, which is a consular document and could easily be remembered a generation later as
evidence of a consular *post*.

**This does not prove the family claim. It is not Pablo Armando, and 1958 is eight years after he died.**

But it establishes something that had never been shown: **there really were Lerenas in the Argentine
diplomatic service.** The surname is rare — about 293 bearers in the whole of Argentina — so a Lerena
carrying an Argentine diplomatic passport is not a common event. Two readings follow, and they point in
opposite directions:

1. **A genuine family connection to Argentine diplomacy** would make the Consul General story far more
   plausible than it currently looks, and would give it a mechanism: a family with a diplomatic tradition.
2. **Or coincidence, plus contamination.** A relative in the diplomatic service, heard of at a distance
   and half-remembered, is exactly the kind of thing that migrates onto the wrong ancestor across two
   generations of retelling.

## And the Gilberto name, for the third time
His son is **Carlos Gilberto**. That pairs the two names of the Argentine Stud Book family — **Gilberto**
Lerena (b. 1854, Uruguay) named a son **Carlos** Augusto Federico (b. 1883 Montevideo). The pairing has
now surfaced three times independently: the 1895 Buenos Aires census, the 1928 CEMLA arrival of an
Argentine Gilberto born about 1879, and now this 1958 diplomat's son.

**This is one family, and it is traceable.** Carlos Gustavo, born 20 January 1915, an Argentine diplomat,
is a documented public figure — far easier to research than a private man. Argentine foreign-ministry
staff lists, the *Guía Diplomática*, and Iranian records of the period would all name him, and his own
service file would name **his father**.

## Next step
Trace **Carlos Gustavo Lerena, b. 20 Jan 1915**, back one generation. If his father or grandfather is the
Gilberto line, the Stud Book family acquires a diplomatic branch — and both family traditions, the horses
*and* the consulship, would finally have a single explanation standing behind them.
