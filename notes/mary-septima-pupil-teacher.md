# Mary Septima Taylor was a pupil teacher

**Source:** *Western Times* (Exeter), **Thursday 4 April 1901, page 7**, "EXETER DIOCESAN BOARD OF
EDUCATION" — the annual examination results. Reprinted in the *North Devon Herald* (Barnstaple),
**Thursday 11 April 1901, page 2**. Full corrected OCR read on findmypast, 9 September 2026.

## What it says

The results are arranged by **Set**, then by class within each set. She appears in:

> **Set II — Monitors over 16 years of age, and Pupil Teachers in their first year — Code of 1899.
> Number examined: 88.**
>
> Class I (7) … Class II (9) …
> **Class III (27)** — … Alice Galliford, 15, Barnstaple Holy Trinity; … Helen Louise Searle, 16; Henry
> Stanbury, 17, Plymouth; Albert J F Stone, 16, Torwood; Harry Strawbridge, 15, Honiton;
> ***Mary Septima Taylor, 16, Northam***; Winnifred S Webby, 16, Honiton.
>
> Passed (30) — Emily Barnes, 15, Barnstaple St Mary Magdalene; …

## What it means

**She was a pupil teacher.** The Exeter Diocesan Board examined the pupil teachers and monitors of the
Church schools of Devon in religious knowledge, and published the results by name, age and parish. To be
in **Set II** in April 1901 she was either a monitor over sixteen or **a pupil teacher in her first
year** — that is, a girl who had stayed on at her village school and was being trained to teach in it,
learning on the job while being examined each year.

She was placed in **Class III of three graded classes**, above the thirty who merely "Passed", out of
eighty-eight examined. Respectable, not distinguished.

Her age is given as **16**, and her parish as **Northam** — she was born there on 17 September 1884 and
baptised there on 30 October 1884, so in April 1901 she was sixteen. Everything checks.

## Why it is worth having

Until now Mary Septima existed in this archive as a set of dates: born Bideford district 1884, married
1911, sailed 1909 and 1935, died 1935. **This is the first thing we know about her as a person** — that at
sixteen she was training to be a schoolteacher in a north Devon village, and sat a diocesan examination
alongside girls from Honiton and Barnstaple.

It also sharpens the shape of her life. A pupil teacher at Northam in 1901; **"Mrs Lerena", crossing from
Cape Town to Southampton with a small son, by February 1909**. Eight years, and the whole distance between
a Devon parish school and an Argentine horseman's household at the Cape. Whatever happened to her happened
in those eight years, and it is the gap this archive still cannot see into.

## A caution
The newspaper OCR is poor and the lists run names together. The *reading* of her own entry is unambiguous
in both papers — "Mary Septima Taylor, 16, Northam" — but the class boundaries around it were reconstructed
from a damaged column, and the neighbouring names should not be quoted without re-checking the image.
